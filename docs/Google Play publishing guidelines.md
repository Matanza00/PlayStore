# Clearing Google Play review in one pass

As of 14 November 2026, an Android app is published and stays published only if five things agree with each other at the same moment: the build (target API 36, App Bundle, 64-bit, Play Billing Library 8+, declared foreground-service types and permissions), the store listing (title 30 / short description 80 / full description 4,000 characters, 512x512 icon, 1024x500 feature graphic, 2-8 screenshots), the App content declarations (privacy policy, ads, app access, content rating, target audience, Data safety, financial features, health apps, plus conditional forms), the live in-app behaviour, and the developer account. The 3-7 day review loop is documented behaviour and not a malfunction: Google's notices are not required to list every violation, every resubmission is a fresh review inside a published envelope of up to seven days or longer, fixes made after a rejection are not sent for review until someone presses **Send for review**, and a non-compliant bundle left active on any testing track makes the resubmission fail ([Enforcement process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234); [Publishing overview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859654); [My app has been removed/rejected](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2477981)). Google offers no expedited review and no API that returns rejection reasons, so the only way to shorten the loop is to stop entering it: audit everything before each submission, freeze the app while it is in review, and fix all tracks at once. Most real rejections are mismatches between two artifacts (Data safety form versus SDK behaviour, listing versus build, declaration versus merged manifest, reviewer credentials versus login flow), which is exactly the kind of fault a custom checker can catch offline. Roughly half of the rule catalogue is automatable from the AAB, a fastlane-style metadata folder and the exported Data safety CSV; login, crash and consent-order checks need a device run; impersonation, misleading claims, limited functionality and audience fit need human or LLM judgement. Two open-source CLIs (GPC `preflight`, Greenlight) already cover manifest-level checks, while nothing on the market diffs the Data safety form against detected SDKs, audits every active track, or tests reviewer credentials. One caution governs the whole document: the researchers read Google's pages through a summarising fetch tool, so policy wording here is close paraphrase and not certified verbatim, and several dates and features are flagged below as unconfirmed or conflicting.

## The review loop is documented behaviour with fifteen official levers

### Why each round takes days and surfaces a different flag

Google publishes one timing statement and it covers the developer's experience exactly: some accounts receive more thorough review, with review times of up to seven days or longer in exceptional cases, and Google recommends leaving a buffer of at least a week between submission and intended go-live ([Publish your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859751); [Publishing overview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859654)). A 3-7 day cycle therefore sits inside the published envelope, and there is no overdue claim to raise until a review passes seven days. Google publishes no SLA for re-review, no statement that resubmissions are prioritised, and no expedited-review mechanism was found on any page the researchers read ([Publishing overview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859654)).

Four documented mechanics turn that single wait into a loop. First, removal and administrative notices are not required to list every violation; the developer is expected to check the whole app and account, and unaddressed violations can draw further enforcement ([Enforcement process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234)). Second, the review clock is counted from the last submitted change, and submitting more changes while a review is running can push the app to the back of the queue ([Publishing overview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859654)). Third, changes are never sent automatically: each one parks in the Publishing overview queue (labelled "Changes not yet sent for review" on some pages and "Changes ready to send for review" on others) until someone clicks **Send for review**, and this applies again after a rejection ([Publish your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859751); [Prepare and roll out a release](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859348)). Fourth, a resubmission after a violation must put the compliant bundle on every track (internal, closed, open, production) and deactivate the non-compliant bundles, otherwise the resubmission fails and live versions can be removed ([My app has been removed/rejected](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2477981)). A fifth trap sits in the permissions flow: a bundle on any track that needs a permissions declaration raises an App content alert and blocks unrelated listing, pricing and distribution changes until resolved, and that declaration review can take several weeks ([Declare permissions for your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9214102)).

Why issues arrive one at a time is not explained by Google anywhere the researchers looked. Developer reports describe three successive rejections on three different policies with roughly a week per round, a reviewer apparently examining an older build, and a stated reason that shifted between messages; these are anecdotal and were read mostly through search snippets ([r/GooglePlayDeveloper mirror](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/GooglePlayDeveloper/comments/1iphy2l/google_play_review_process_is_broken); [r/androiddev mirror](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/androiddev/comments/1cllwr5/i_cant_figure_out_the_reason_for_the_rejection)). The researchers' working explanation, labelled as inference, is a staged pipeline (automated artifact checks, then declaration checks, then a human walk-through) that issues a notice at the first blocking finding, so later stages never run until earlier ones pass. Two sequences recur in the reports: an app-access failure, then Data safety or prominent disclosure once the reviewer gets in, then payments or subscriptions; and a permissions or foreground-service flag that returns because an old bundle on a test track still declares it.

The loop is also not free. Google's enforcement page says suspension can result from repeated rejections or removals as well as from egregious violations, and each suspension counts as a strike against the account ([Enforcement process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234)). The testing-requirements page states plainly that submitting non-compliant apps in the expectation that reviewers will identify the issues leads to rejections, delays and longer appeals ([App testing requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)). With four apps on one account, every exploratory submission adds account-level risk; Google publishes no threshold for how many rejections trigger a suspension. For scale, a vendor relay of Google's 2025 transparency report gives 21,461,250 submissions reviewed and 1,692,467 rejected (about 7.9%), with 806,795 of those rejections later approved after a fix and only about 8.2% of decided appeals reversed; the primary report was not opened, so treat the figures as second-hand ([TesterBee](https://testerbee.com/blog/google-play-app-rejection-rate-2026-the-real-numbers)).

The same post announced a release status API to check whether a release is approved and published; no reference documentation for it was found, although an `applications.tracks.releases.list` method describing releases as ready to send, in review, approved, not approved or available does appear in the API reference ([Android Developers Blog, 7 May 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/05/making-it-easier-to-build-publish-safer-apps.html); [releases.list](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/applications.tracks.releases/list)).

### Account type and the 12-tester gate decide who reaches production

A Play Console account costs a one-time **US$25**, requires the owner to be at least 18, and comes as personal or organization. Personal accounts must verify a real Android device through the Play Console mobile app; organization accounts need a **D-U-N-S number**, which Google says can take up to 30 days to obtain (the developer-verification FAQ says up to 28) ([Get started with Play Console](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435); [Required account information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312); [Verification FAQ](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq)). The account type is also a content gate: the Developer Program Policy requires an organization account for financial products and services, health and medical apps, apps approved to use `VpnService`, and government apps ([Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)). Public profiles differ: individuals show legal name, country and developer email, with the full address added if they monetize; organizations show legal name, legal address, developer email and phone ([Required account information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312)).

Personal accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a closed test with at least **12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days** before applying for production; a tester who opts out and back in restarts their 14 days, and the same accounts cannot use open testing or pre-registration first. The application is a three-part questionnaire (the closed test, the app, production readiness) that Google usually answers within seven days; the two named reasons for being told to keep testing are fewer than 12 opted-in testers and insufficient tester engagement ([App testing requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465); [Set up an open, closed, or internal test](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334)). Google does not define sufficient engagement numerically, and whether the requirement applies per app or once per account was not explicit in the extract the researchers obtained. Organization accounts and older personal accounts are exempt by construction.

Accounts also lapse and cascade. An account with apps is treated as inactive when it is over a year old, has fewer than 1,000 combined lifetime installs, has unverified phone and contact email, and has not used Play Console in 180 days; reminders go out 60, 30 and 7 days before closure ([Inactive developer accounts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11605267)). Termination removes every app and permanently suspends related accounts ([Enforcement process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234)). Separately, Android developer verification has been enforced since **30 September 2026** for installs on certified devices in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand, with global expansion in 2027 on an unpublished date; for Play developers using Play App Signing, about 99% of package names were registered automatically and the remaining task is to check the Play Console Home page ([Android developer verification](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification); [Verification guide for Play Console](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/google-play-console)).

### Tracks, statuses and release mechanics define what counts as a review

Review covers the release artifact, the store listing and the App content declarations together, and everything queued in Publishing overview goes as one submission, so one bad item can reject the batch. The Console exposes three status layers: app status (Draft through Production, Removed, Suspended), update status (In review, Update rejected, App rejected, Changes not yet sent for review) and item status. "App rejected" applies only to a draft being published for the first time; "Update rejected" means one or more changes do not comply while the prior version stays live ([Publish your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859751)). Full and staged rollouts, store listing changes including custom listings and experiments, app content, category and tester configuration all pass through the review gate; raising a staged rollout to 100%, release notes, device exclusions, tester-list membership, in-app products and price changes do not ([Publishing overview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859654)).

| Track or mechanism | Capacity | Review | Notes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Internal testing | Up to 100 testers per app | Generally none; retroactive review possible | Delivered within minutes; usable before app setup is complete | [Testing tracks](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334) |
| Closed testing | Email lists up to 200 per account and 50 per track, 2,000 testers each; Google Groups unlimited | Reviewed | Multiple closed tracks allowed | [Testing tracks](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334) |
| Open testing | Unlimited; an optional cap must be at least 1,000 | Reviewed | Visible on Play; signing key becomes fixed once on an open track | [Testing tracks](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334); [Prepare and roll out a release](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859348) |
| Internal app sharing | 100 users per link; links expire after 60 days | None | Any signing key, debuggable builds allowed, version codes need not be unique | [Internal app sharing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844679) |
| Production | All users in selected countries | Reviewed | First release cannot be staged | [Prepare and roll out a release](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859348) |

Release notes are capped at 500 Unicode characters per language and must not be promotional. Staged rollouts apply only to updates and never advance automatically; a rollout can be halted, and since 2025 even a fully rolled-out release can be halted on any track except internal testing so that the previous release serves new users (the 100%-halt detail was read from a search snippet). There is no rollback to an arbitrary older binary: the fix path is a new release with a higher version code, which is reviewed again ([Prepare and roll out a release](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859348); [Staged rollouts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6346149); [Halt a fully rolled-out release](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16285429)). Pre-registration campaigns are limited to 90 days per country, and missing the launch in any country ends the campaign everywhere ([Pre-registration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9084187)).

### The enforcement ladder runs from rejection to termination, with one appeal per action

A **rejection** leaves the prior version live and does not affect account standing. A **removal** takes the app and prior versions down until a compliant update, keeping users, statistics and ratings. A **suspension** removes the app, forfeits users, statistics and ratings, and counts as a strike. Limited visibility and limited regions restrict discovery without affecting standing. A restricted account cannot publish, and **termination** removes all apps and permanently suspends related accounts. Warnings carry a number of days after which the app is removed ([Enforcement process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234); [My app has been removed/rejected](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2477981)). The violation itself is stated in three places: Play Console Notifications, the email from Google Play, and the Policy status page, which shows an Appeal button for suspended apps ([Check your app's policy status](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9842754)).

Appeals are limited to one per enforcement action, are granted where an error was made, and receive replies only in Chinese, English, Japanese and Korean ([My app has been removed/rejected](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2477981)). Since 28 January 2026 an account-termination appeal must be filed within 180 days of termination, and Google says nearly all successful appeals resolve within six months ([Account termination appeal time limit](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16659089)). The researchers' advice, consistent with the 8% reversal figure above, is to fix and resubmit unless the reviewer is demonstrably wrong (for example, valid credentials that the reviewer reported as failing), and to attach evidence when appealing because the single appeal should not be spent on a borderline case.

### Bundle format, signing, size and version codes

New apps have had to publish as Android App Bundles since August 2021, and Play does not support OBB expansion files for bundles; large titles use Play Asset Delivery or Play Feature Delivery ([Android Developers Blog, June 2021](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2021/06/the-future-of-android-app-bundles-is.html); [App Bundle FAQ](https://developer.android.com/guide/app-bundle/faq)). Size is measured as compressed download size computed by Play on upload: **500 MB** for the base module, 500 MB per feature module, 1.5 GB per asset pack, 4 GB across all modules and install-time packs, 30 GB across on-demand and fast-follow packs, and 34 GB in total. Apps above 1 GB need minSdk 21 or higher, apps above 200 MB trigger a non-blocking mobile-data dialog, and the legacy APK limit is 100 MB ([Play Console Help 9859372](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859372?hl=en)).

The developer holds an upload key (RSA 2048-bit or larger) that Google can reset if lost; Google holds the app signing key, and new apps are auto-enrolled in quantum-ready hybrid signing with an RSA 4096 key plus an ML-DSA-65 key. Hybrid-signed apps expose three certificate fingerprints, all of which must be registered with API providers that key on the signing certificate, and the app signing key can only be changed before a release reaches open testing or production ([Play App Signing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9842756?hl=en)). `versionCode` must be a positive integer no greater than **2,100,000,000** and can never be reused ([Version your app](https://developer.android.com/studio/publish/versioning)). Upload rejections for `android:debuggable="true"`, debug-certificate signing, `android:testOnly="true"` and `com.example` package prefixes are widely reported by CI vendors but no official page stating them was retrieved; check them anyway ([Codemagic](https://docs.codemagic.io/troubleshooting/common-google-play-errors/)). The package name is unique and permanent and cannot be deleted or reused ([Create and set up your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859152?hl=en)).

### Native code must be 64-bit and 16 KB-aligned

Every 32-bit ABI shipped must be paired with its 64-bit counterpart (`armeabi-v7a` with `arm64-v8a`, `x86` with `x86_64`); pure Java/Kotlin apps comply with no action, and RenderScript `.bc` bitcode from build-tools older than 21.0.0 breaks compliance ([Support 64-bit architectures](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/64-bit)). For 16 KB, any app with native code that targets API 35 or higher is in scope, which now means every app with a `.so` file because the target floor is 36. Compliance needs AGP 8.5.1 or higher with uncompressed shared libraries (or legacy compressed packaging), NDK r28 or higher (or the explicit `-Wl,-z,max-page-size=16384` linker flag on older NDKs), and rebuilt third-party prebuilts. Verification is mechanical: every LOAD segment of each `arm64-v8a` and `x86_64` library must show alignment of 2**14 or higher in `llvm-objdump -p`, `zipalign -v -c -P 16 4` must verify, and `bundletool dump config` must report `PAGE_ALIGNMENT_16K` ([Support 16 KB page sizes](https://developer.android.com/guide/practices/page-sizes)).

Framework version usually decides this. React Native added 16 KB support in 0.77 (Expo SDK 53, `expo@53.0.14` or newer) and targets API 36 by default from 0.81; Unity supports 16 KB on 2021, 2022 and Unity 6 and needs IL2CPP with ARM64 for 64-bit; lowering targetSdk to 34 is not a way out ([Expo FYI](https://github.com/expo/fyi/blob/main/android-16kb-page-sizes.md); [React Native 0.81](https://reactnative.dev/blog/2025/08/12/react-native-0.81); [Unity on Android](https://developer.android.com/games/engines/unity/unity-on-android)). The first Flutter stable release with a default target of 36 and 16 KB-aligned engine binaries, and the Capacitor major that defaults to API 36, were not confirmed.

### Code behaviours that policy bans outright

The Device and Network Abuse policy converts into concrete code bans. An app may update itself only through Google Play; it may not download executable code (dex, JAR, `.so`) from anywhere else, with an exclusion for code that runs in a virtual machine or interpreter such as JavaScript in a WebView, provided runtime-loaded interpreted code does not enable policy violations. Listed violations include interfering with other apps' ads, installing other apps without consent, a WebView with an added JavaScript interface that loads untrusted content, and full-screen intents used to force interaction with ads ([Device and Network Abuse](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888379?hl=en)). The researchers infer that over-the-air JavaScript bundle updates (Expo Updates, CodePush-style) fall under the interpreter exclusion only while the delivered code does not change what the app is. The verbatim policy text could not be extracted, so confirm wording at the cited URL.

Apps targeting Android 14 or higher must declare an `android:foregroundServiceType` and the matching `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_<TYPE>` permission for every foreground service, across the types camera, connectedDevice, dataSync, health, location, mediaPlayback, mediaProcessing, mediaProjection, microphone, phoneCall, remoteMessaging, shortService, specialUse and systemExempted, and declare them on the App content page ([Foreground service types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)). Apps targeting API 31 or higher must set `android:exported` explicitly on every component with an intent filter and give every `PendingIntent` an explicit mutability flag ([Target API requirements](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)).

Google scans every upload and rescans published apps under the App Security Improvement programme. Campaigns with remediation deadlines, after which updates can be blocked, cover intent redirection, JavaScript interface injection, scheme hijacking, cross-app scripting, file-based XSS, SQL injection, path traversal, insecure hostname verification, fragment injection, unsafe `TrustManager` and `WebView` SSL error handlers, exposed FCM server keys, and vulnerable versions of libpng, libjpeg-turbo, OpenSSL, GnuTLS, Apache Cordova and several ad SDKs. Warning-only campaigns cover implicit PendingIntents, implicit internal intents, unsafe encryption modes, zip path traversal and embedded credentials (AWS keys, OAuth tokens, keystore files) ([App security improvement program](https://developer.android.com/google/play/asi)). No new named campaign has appeared on the public page since February 2022. The Malware and Mobile Unwanted Software policies add behaviour categories that scanners flag, including a numeric hostile-downloader rule (at least 5% of at least 500 observed downloads being harmful apps) and a requirement that monitoring apps declare the `IsMonitoringTool` flag ([Malware policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888380?hl=en); [Mobile Unwanted Software](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9970222?hl=en)).

### What changes when the build targets API 35, 36 and 37

Moving to targetSdk 36 removes the edge-to-edge opt-out (`windowOptOutEdgeToEdgeEnforcement` is disabled on Android 16 devices), enables predictive back so that `onBackPressed` is no longer called, and makes orientation, resizability and aspect-ratio locks ineffective on displays with smallest width of 600dp or more. A temporary opt-out exists through the `PROPERTY_COMPAT_ALLOW_RESTRICTED_RESIZABILITY` manifest property, and it stops working at API 37; games are exempt via `android:appCategory` ([Android 16 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16)). API 35 already enforced edge-to-edge by default, capped `dataSync` and `mediaProcessing` foreground services at six hours per 24, restricted foreground-service launches from `BOOT_COMPLETED`, and disallowed TLS 1.0 and 1.1 ([Android 15 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15)). API 37 will require the `ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK` runtime permission for LAN access, read-only files for `System.load()`, and will withhold OTP-bearing SMS from most apps for three hours ([Android 17 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-17)). Flutter's Android 15 opt-out can crash on Android 16 and should live only in a `res/values-35/` variant ([Flutter breaking change](https://docs.flutter.dev/release/breaking-changes/default-systemuimode-edge-to-edge)). WebView shells are the most exposed to edge-to-edge regressions, by the researchers' reading.

### SDK versions and vitals keep an app published after approval

The developer is responsible for every bundled SDK, and data an SDK collects is treated as collected by the app ([SDK requirements overview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13326895?hl=en)). Play Console identifies SDK versions from the dependency metadata in the bundle. A version that violates policy can cause new releases to be rejected; a version with critical issues allows one further release and must be replaced within **90 days**; versions reported outdated by the SDK owner generate a warning ([Using third-party SDKs in your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10358880)). Whether an outdated (as opposed to critical) version ever blocks a release was read differently by two researchers and remains unconfirmed.

Android vitals determine visibility once the app is live. The overall bad-behaviour thresholds are a **1.09% user-perceived crash rate** and a **0.47% user-perceived ANR rate**, with 8% per phone model, and excessive partial wake locks in more than 5% of sessions; Play evaluates a 28-day window and can reduce visibility or show a warning on the listing ([Android vitals](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/vitals); [Excessive partial wake locks](https://developer.android.com/google/play/vitals/excessive-wakelock)). From February 2027, memory usage by device RAM tier, bitmap memory (200 MB in background, 400 MB cached) and a DEX-optimization minimum of 25% for apps with more than 10 MB of DEX code (50 MB for games) also affect visibility. The researchers infer that this makes R8 effectively mandatory for larger apps, which matters for React Native templates that ship with minification off.

## Privacy mismatches drive the largest share of flags

Google publishes no ranked list of submission-rejection reasons. The evidence for putting privacy first is indirect: data protection and privacy is the largest category of post-publication removals in Google's 2025 transparency data (876,316 removals, relayed by a vendor blog), Google's own description of pre-review checks names improper use of credentials and permissions as common rejection reasons, and Google reports blocking over 255,000 apps from excessive access to sensitive data in 2025 ([TesterBee](https://testerbee.com/blog/google-play-app-rejection-rate-2026-the-real-numbers); [Google security blog, 19 Feb 2026](https://blog.google/security/keeping-google-play-android-app-ecosystem-safe-2025/)). The ordering is the researchers' synthesis and not measured data.

### Every app needs a privacy policy and a consent flow that precedes the permission prompt

The User Data policy requires a privacy policy in the Play Console field and inside the app, including for apps that access no personal or sensitive data. The page must sit on an active, publicly accessible, non-geofenced URL, must not be a PDF, must be non-editable, must be clearly labelled as a privacy policy, and must name either the developer entity shown on the store listing or the app. Its content must cover developer information and a privacy contact, the types of personal and sensitive data accessed, collected, used and shared and with whom, secure handling procedures, and retention and deletion ([User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)). Personal and sensitive data may be used only for purposes the user reasonably expects, must travel over modern cryptography, and may never be sold.

Where collection would not be within a user's reasonable expectation (background collection is the standard example), the app must show a **prominent disclosure** inside the app, in normal use and not buried in a menu, describing the data and its use, not bundled with unrelated disclosures and not located only in the privacy policy. Consent must follow immediately, come before any runtime permission request, require an affirmative action, and never treat navigating away or an auto-dismissing message as agreement ([User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)). Requesting the runtime permission before showing the disclosure is a listed violation, which is why consent order is a device-run check. Persistent identifiers such as IMEI and IMSI may not be linked to other personal data or to resettable identifiers, and the advertising ID may not be linked to persistent identifiers or email for ads or analytics ([SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374)). Since the 15 July 2026 announcement, Google states that User Data requirements also apply to third-party AI integrations; listing the model provider as a data recipient and gating the transfer on consent is a reasonable reading drawn from a secondary article and not Google's own checklist ([Policy announcement 15 Jul 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731); [DEV Community summary](https://dev.to/mr_manushukla/google-plays-july-2026-policy-update-5-changes-to-ship-before-the-31-august-deadline-3bie)).

### The Data safety form must match the app plus every SDK

The form is required for every app on closed, open or production tracks, including apps that collect nothing; only apps active solely on internal testing are exempt. "Collected" means transmitted off the device, including by SDKs and by a WebView the app controls, and pseudonymous data counts. "Shared" means transferred to a third party, including server-to-server. Data processed only on-device, end-to-end encrypted data the developer cannot read, and transfers to a service provider acting on the developer's behalf are excluded ([Data safety form](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)). The taxonomy has fourteen categories (location, personal info, financial info, health and fitness, messages, photos and videos, audio files, files and docs, calendar, contacts, app activity, web browsing, app info and performance, device or other IDs) and seven purposes (app functionality, analytics, developer communications, advertising or marketing, fraud prevention and security, personalization, account management). "Encrypted in transit" may be declared only if all user data from the app and its SDKs is encrypted, and the form's data-deletion questions carry the account-deletion URL.

Google says its review does not verify the accuracy of declarations but that it may detect discrepancies between the form and app behaviour and then block updates or remove the app; it does not publish how. A real notice from 2024 named a version code and a specific undeclared data type (approximate location), which shows that detection is tied to individual bundles ([Data safety form](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469); [commons-app issue #5708](https://github.com/commons-app/apps-android-commons/issues/5708)). The form exports and imports as CSV, and the same CSV is what the write-only `applications.dataSafety` API method uploads; it cannot be read back through the API ([dataSafety method](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/applications/dataSafety)). The practical pre-submission test is therefore a three-way diff: merged-manifest permissions plus SDK inventory, against the Data safety answers, against the privacy-policy text.

### Apps with accounts need an in-app deletion path and a web deletion URL

Any app that lets users create an account, in-app or by sending them to an external sign-up flow, must offer a discoverable in-app path to start deletion and a working web link entered in the Data safety form. The web resource must load without error, reference the app or developer name as shown on the listing, make the request route prominent, and not require reinstalling the app; deactivation or freezing does not count, associated data must be deleted, and anything retained for security, fraud or legal reasons must be disclosed in the privacy policy. Permanently private apps and enterprise device-management apps are exempt, and enforcement including removal has been possible since 31 May 2024 ([Account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)).

### Sensitive permissions need an approved declaration or must leave the merged manifest

The general rule is that permissions touching sensitive information must be necessary for core functionality promoted in the listing, requested in context, and never used to sell data ([Permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170)). Because SDK manifests merge into the app manifest, every check below must run on the merged manifest from the built bundle.

### Children's data and the 2027 permission schedule

Apps that solely target children must not transmit the advertising ID, SIM serial, build serial, BSSID, MAC, SSID, IMEI or IMSI, should not request `AD_ID` when targeting API 33+, may not request location permission, and may contain only SDKs approved for child-directed services; mixed-audience apps must not transmit those identifiers from children or users of unknown age ([Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)). EU consent is enforced through Google's ads products and not through a Play form: serving personalised ads in the EEA, UK and Switzerland requires a Google-certified consent management platform integrated with the IAB TCF ([AdMob consent requirements](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/13554116)).

Four permission changes land on **27 January 2027** according to Google's live Policy Deadlines table: `READ_CONTACTS` is reserved for apps that cannot function with the Contact Picker (declaration required, scoped to apps targeting API 37+), precise location for one-time user-initiated actions must use the Android location button, `READ_CALL_LOG` can no longer be used for phone-call account verification, and geofencing stops being an approved foreground-service use. The AppFunctions policy follows on 14 April 2027, and declarations for `ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK` and `MANAGE_CONTACTS` on 25 August 2027 ([Policy Deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780); [Policy preview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16909972)). A secondary article gives 28 October 2026 for the contacts and location changes; the live Google pages show 27 January 2027 and are the better evidence, although no Google statement announcing a postponement was found. Pre-review checks for contacts and location permissions were due to start on 27 October 2026, so the checker should already warn on them ([Android Developers Blog, April 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/04/giving-users-clearer-choice-and-everyone-a-safer-more-trusted-app-ecosystem.html)). The Play Age Signals API is optional from Play's side, and its data may be used only to provide age-appropriate experiences, never for advertising, analytics or profiling ([Play Age Signals overview](https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/overview)).

## Content and behaviour rules are judgement calls with mechanical triggers

### The policy map, and which parts a machine can test

The Developer Program Policy effective 29 October 2026 is organised as Restricted Content (child endangerment, inappropriate content, age-restricted content, financial services, real-money gambling, illegal activities, user-generated content, health, blockchain, AI-generated content), Intellectual Property, Privacy/Deception/Device Abuse, Malware, Impersonation, Mobile Unwanted Software, Monetization and Ads, Store Listing and Promotion, Spam/Functionality/User Experience, Families, Enforcement and Play Console Requirements. Since 15 July 2026 some sections carry standardised numbering such as Deceptive Behavior 2.2.1. The policies apply to anything the app displays or links to, including ads, linked user content, the developer name and the listed website ([Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346); [Policy announcement 15 Jul 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731)). Most content rules are absolute prohibitions that need human or LLM review: sexual content and profanity (with an educational, documentary, scientific or artistic exception), hate speech, gratuitous violence, violent extremism, capitalising on sensitive events, bullying, facilitating sales of firearms and listed accessories, marijuana sales regardless of legality, tobacco sales, illegal activities, intellectual-property infringement and impersonation. The mechanically testable obligations cluster in declarations, in-app safety features and listing disclosures, which the following subsections cover.

### Spam and limited functionality have no numeric thresholds

An app may not have the primary purpose of driving affiliate traffic or of providing a WebView of a website without permission from the site's owner, may not merely replicate other apps, and a developer with several highly similar apps of small content volume is told to consider one aggregated app ([Spam](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034?hl=en)). The rule usually called Minimum Functionality is now titled Limited Functionality and Content: static apps without app-specific functionality (text-only or PDF apps), apps with very little content (a single wallpaper) and apps designed to do nothing are not allowed. Broken Functionality covers apps that do not install, install but do not load, or load but do not respond ([Functionality, Content, and User Experience](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898783?hl=en)). Google publishes no minimum screen count, content volume, crash rate or similarity score, so borderline cases are decided by a reviewer. Wrapping the developer's own site satisfies the permission limb of the WebView rule and can still fail Limited Functionality; the text treats them as separate tests. A developer running four apps from one template should read the repetitive-content rule and Google's white-label guidance closely ([Best practices for white label developers](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15884185?hl=en)).

### Listing claims must be demonstrable in the submitted build

Deceptive Behavior requires that all metadata accurately disclose functionality, bans claims of impossible functions even as a prank, bans hidden, dormant or undocumented features and techniques to evade review, and requires consent and easy reversal for device-settings changes. Listed violations include core functionality that varies by geography or device without prominent listing disclosure, significant changes between versions without updating the listing, and downloading extra resources without first prompting the user and disclosing the size. The 29 October 2026 update added an explicit statement that developers and apps may not manipulate the app review process; its exact wording was not retrieved ([Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346); [Policy announcement 29 Oct 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18100766)). Remote-config-driven changes after approval are a direct risk under these clauses, by the researchers' reading. Manipulated media needs user-facing disclaimers or watermarks, and an app conveying government information without affiliation must show verifiable sources and a non-affiliation statement in the store description ([Government information apps](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9514050?hl=en)).

### Target audience triggers the Families rules

Every app declares target age groups (5 and under, 6-8, 9-12, 13-15, 16-17, 18 and over). Including any under-13 group, or being judged child-appealing by Google, triggers the Families Policy Requirements: child-appropriate content, no bare WebView or affiliate funnel, accurate Console answers, restricted data practices, approved SDKs only, AR safety warnings, adult-gated social features, and legal compliance with COPPA and GDPR. Mixed-audience apps need a neutral age screen, meaning free entry of a birth date without presets or hints about a required age ([Manage target audience](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en); [Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)). A non-child app with youthful animation or young characters in its listing can be rejected until the art changes or the audience declaration does. Selecting 18 and over as the only group enables Restrict Minor Access, which is mandatory for real-money gambling, core dating or matchmaking, random-chat and anonymous-communication apps. Anonymous-chat, random-chat, social and dating apps must also self-certify against the Child Safety Standards policy: published CSAE prohibitions, in-app reporting, action on CSAM, legal compliance and a named contact; the extension to anonymous and random chat took effect 26 August 2026 ([Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346); [Policy Deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780)).

### Regulated categories combine a declaration, an account type and listing disclosures

Financial rules are the most detailed. Every app on the account must complete the Financial features declaration even when it has none, and apps cannot be updated until it is done ([Policy announcement 30 Oct 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16550159)). Personal-loan apps must use the Finance category and disclose in metadata the minimum and maximum repayment period, the maximum APR and a representative total-cost example; loans requiring full repayment in **60 days or less** are prohibited; and personal-loan, loan-facilitation, loan-calculator and earned-wage-access apps may not hold `READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE`, `READ_MEDIA_IMAGES`, `READ_MEDIA_VIDEO`, `READ_CONTACTS`, `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`, `READ_PHONE_NUMBERS`, `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES` or `WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE`. Country rules add licence proof: a US APR cap below 36%, the RBI digital-lending list in India, OJK licences in Indonesia, SEC registration and Certificate of Authority numbers in the Philippines, FCCPC approval in Nigeria, Central Bank licensing in Kenya, a Bank of Thailand or Ministry of Finance licence at interest of 15% or more in Thailand, and in Pakistan SECP approval with only one digital lending app per NBFC lender ([Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)). Binary options apps are banned, cryptocurrency exchanges and wallets need jurisdiction-specific licences or one of the "Additional Options" added in October 2026, and on-device mining is prohibited ([Cryptocurrency exchanges and software wallets](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16329703)).

All developers must complete the Health apps declaration, including for apps with no health features. Health apps that are not regulated medical devices need a disclaimer in the description that the app is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition, must disclose required external hardware or supported device models, and regulated devices receive a Medical Device label ([Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346); [Health apps declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14738291)). Real-money gambling is limited to approved, licensed operators in select countries, with free download, no Play billing, an Adults Only rating and geo-blocking; every other app may not let users stake money for prizes of real-world value ([Real-Money Gambling](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)). News and magazine apps must self-declare with a contact URL, entity and aggregator status, attribute sources and update content regularly; undeclared in-scope apps faced removal after 27 May 2026, and the "not more than 30 days old" freshness standard comes from a search excerpt and should be verified ([How to declare a news and magazine app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16189314?hl=en); [News and magazine requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10523915?hl=en)).

### User-generated and AI-generated content need in-app report controls

Any content that users contribute and other users can see counts as UGC, which includes comments, reviews and public profiles. Such apps must have users accept terms before posting, must define prohibited content, and must provide an in-app system to report and block objectionable content and users, scaled to the type: blocking for direct messaging, report and block for public content ([Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)). Apps with generative AI as a feature must prevent generation of restricted content and must let users report or flag offensive AI output without leaving the app; Google's best-practices article points developers to its Secure AI Framework and the OWASP GenAI red-teaming guide ([Best practices for generative AI apps](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16353813)). The researchers infer that an email link which exits the app would not satisfy the in-app requirement. Whether the AI section still lists violation examples and exclusions in the October 2026 text could not be confirmed, and no official page describes a dedicated AI-content declaration form.

## Monetization: Play Billing stays the global default while regional programmes multiply

### When Google Play's billing system is mandatory

Paid downloads and in-app digital goods or services (items, subscriptions, ad-free versions, cloud services, app functionality) must use Google Play's billing system unless the Payments policy's Section 3, 8 or 9 applies. Section 3 lists what must not use it: physical goods and services, bill remittances, peer-to-peer payments, auctions, tax-exempt donations and licensed gambling. Outside the programme regions, an app may not lead users to another payment method through its listing, in-app promotions, WebViews, buttons or links. Virtual currency may be used only in the title where it was bought, and loot boxes must disclose odds before and close to the purchase ([Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)). Google's explainer adds the working cases: consumption-only apps where nothing can be bought are allowed, tips are exempt only if the creator receives 100% and the tipper receives no digital perk, one-to-one non-replayable services between individuals are exempt, and the policy applies to purchases facilitated inside AI or agentic experiences ([Understanding the Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)).

The regional picture changed sharply. In the United States, Google stopped requiring its billing system and stopped enforcing anti-steering on 29 October 2025 to comply with the Epic v. Google injunction, then launched US alternative billing and external content links programmes on 9 December 2025; enrolled developers must report transactions and pay service fees from 1 October 2026, with external-link download reporting due 1 February 2027 ([US policy changes](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en)). A billing choice programme began in the EEA and UK on 30 June 2026 and in Australia and Japan on 30 September 2026, requiring Play billing to be offered alongside the alternative ([Billing choice program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17161464?hl=en)). Court approval of the March 2026 Epic settlement was not confirmed from a primary source. For users everywhere else, including South Asia, none of these programmes apply yet, so any external payment link for digital goods must be geo-gated to enrolled regions or it risks an anti-steering rejection.

### Subscription screens must carry every material term without an extra tap

The offer screen must itself disclose the cost, billing frequency, auto-renewal terms, what is included and whether a subscription is required to use the app, with no additional user action needed to see them. Free trials must state duration, conversion price and timing, and that the user can cancel beforehand. The app must disclose how to manage or cancel and include an easy online cancellation method in account settings, such as a link to Play's subscription centre. Listed violations include an annual plan that most prominently shows a monthly price, incompletely localised pricing, a product named "Free Trial" that auto-renews, a flow where users can subscribe accidentally, and subscriptions with no recurring value ([Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)). From **27 January 2027**, subscriptions with ongoing eligibility-based discounts such as student plans must disclose eligibility criteria, the verification process and post-eligibility pricing ([Policy Deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780)). Opt-in price increases need user agreement and at least 30 days' notice ([Understanding subscriptions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12154973?hl=en)).

On the implementation side, the latest Play Billing Library is 9.1.0 (18 June 2026), and version 8 removed `querySkuDetailsAsync()` and `queryPurchaseHistory()`. Play detects the library through the `com.google.android.play.billingclient.version` meta-data in the merged manifest, and the version requirement matters only for builds that request `com.android.vending.BILLING` ([PBL release notes](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/release-notes); [PBL deprecation FAQ](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/deprecation-faq)). Every purchase must be acknowledged or consumed within **three days** or it is refunded automatically, entitlement is granted only in the PURCHASED state, and the app must call `queryPurchasesAsync()` to restore purchases made elsewhere ([Integrate the Play Billing Library](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate)). Wrappers such as react-native-iap, Flutter's in_app_purchase and RevenueCat pin a billing library version transitively, so a checker should resolve the transitive version and not trust the wrapper's version number.

### Ads are part of the app and must behave inside it

Ads must suit the app's content rating, be clearly identifiable, display only within the serving app, and be dismissible without penalty. Full-screen interstitials that appear unexpectedly (for example at the start of a level), full-screen video before the loading screen, and any full-screen interstitial not closeable after **15 seconds** are prohibited; rewarded ads the user opts into are outside that rule. Repeated interstitials after consecutive user actions fall under the made-for-ads rule, lock-screen monetisation is banned unless the app is a lock screen, and ad fraud is prohibited ([Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en); [Ad Fraud](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9969955?hl=en)). The "Contains ads" declaration must be Yes whenever an ad SDK, house ad or native sponsored content exists, and misstating it can lead to suspension ([Prepare your app for review](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455)). For children or users of unknown age, ads must come only from Families Self-Certified Ads SDK versions, interest-based advertising is banned, ads must be closeable after 5 seconds, interstitials on app launch are prohibited and so is more than one ad placement per page ([Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)).

AdMob's own rules are stricter than Play's and often coincide with Play's disruptive-ads examples: no interstitials on app load or exit, no more than one interstitial per two user actions, no Google-served ads on screens without content such as login, error or exit screens, no cash rewards for rewarded ads, and an `app-ads.txt` file at the root of the developer website named in the listing ([Disallowed interstitial implementations](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201362?hl=en); [Ads on screens without publisher content](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11112688?hl=en); [app-ads.txt](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9363762?hl=en)). AdMob violations limit ad serving and do not by themselves remove an app from Play.

## Store listing and App content: exact limits and mandatory declarations

### Text limits and the Metadata policy

The title is limited to **30 characters**, the short description to **80**, the full description to **4,000**, release notes to 500 per language and graphic alt text to 140; full-width and half-width characters count the same ([Create and set up your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859152?hl=en); [Add preview assets](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151?hl=en)). The Metadata policy forbids, in the title, icon and developer name: emojis, emoticons and repeated special characters; ALL CAPS unless it is the brand; misleading symbols; ranking or performance claims such as "#1", "App of the year" or "Popular"; price and promotion text such as "10% off" or "free for limited time only"; and Play programme claims such as "Editor's choice" or "New". Descriptions may not contain unattributed or anonymous testimonials, comparisons with other apps or brands, or blocks and lists of keywords, and repetitive or irrelevant keywords can lead to suspension ([Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en)). The preview-assets page extends similar wording limits to the short description and graphics (no "Best", "Top", "Sale", "Million Downloads", and no calls to action such as "Download now"), and splits its rules into Requirements, where failure can mean removal or suspension, and Highly recommended items, where failure only limits promotion. A validator should therefore emit two severities. The listing must suit a general audience regardless of the app's rating, and the same policies apply to every translation. A developer-name character limit (often reported as 50) could not be confirmed on any official page.

Screenshots must show the actual in-app experience, keep taglines to no more than 20% of the image, and avoid device frames, ranking or price claims and calls to action. A feature shown or described in the listing but absent from the app can cause rejection ([Helpful tips to get published](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15191715?hl=en)). A failing form-factor asset such as a TV screenshot can block listing updates for all devices until fixed or removed ([TV app quality](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/tv-app-quality)). No per-screenshot file-size limit is stated for non-XR screenshots, and no pixel values are published for feature-graphic safe zones. None of the 2025-2026 policy announcements changed listing limits or asset dimensions.

### Declarations every app must complete

The researchers' reading of the App content page is that eight declarations are mandatory for every app even when the answer is "none": privacy policy, ads, app access, content rating, target audience and content, Data safety, financial features and health apps. Conditional declarations cover news and magazine apps, government apps, permissions forms, and the app-level trader confirmation ([Prepare your app for review](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455?hl=en); [Financial features declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13849271?hl=en); [Health apps declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14738291?hl=en)). The content rating comes from the IARC questionnaire and is issued per rating authority; the 15 July 2026 announcement states that unrated apps are not allowed, the questionnaire must be retaken after material content changes, and misrepresentation can cause removal or suspension ([Content ratings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/188189?hl=en)). Pre-review checks block sending for review when mandatory declarations are missing ([Pre-review checks](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14807773)).

App access is where the most avoidable rejections arise. If any part of the app is restricted by credentials, membership, location or other authentication, the developer must supply everything needed: at most five instruction sets, credentials that are always accessible, reusable and valid regardless of reviewer location, a bypass for OTP or two-step verification, instructions in English, a static URL for QR-gated access, and a route past any paywall. An expired password can prevent review and get the app rejected, and the fix needs no new build: update the details, save, and send for review ([Sign-in details requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15748846?hl=en); [Prepare your app for review](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455?hl=en)).

A new account-level declaration arrives now. All active developer accounts must declare trader or non-trader status under EEA and UK law regardless of where their apps are distributed; the declaration opened in November 2026 with a **1 February 2027** deadline for existing accounts, missing it can block updates and remove apps globally, and from 1 March 2027 Play displays trader contact details, D-U-N-S and a Terms of Service link per app ([Trader status declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17594752?hl=en)). Since 22 July 2026, US listings are also shared with third-party US Android stores by default unless the developer opts out in Play apps settings ([Third-party US stores notice](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17187609?hl=en)).

### Links, categories and contact details

The privacy-policy URL rules above translate into HTTP checks: live, public, valid certificate, worldwide, no login, an ordinary HTML page and not a PDF or editable document. Android App Links additionally need `https://<domain>/.well-known/assetlinks.json` served as `application/json` over HTTPS with no redirects on every host in the manifest, containing the package name and the SHA-256 fingerprint of the Play App Signing key, which usually differs from the local keystore ([Configure website associations](https://developer.android.com/training/app-links/configure-assetlinks)). Each app has one category and at most five tags, which must be obviously relevant; a support email is mandatory on the listing while website and phone are optional there; and website ownership may need Search Console verification, which social-media profile URLs cannot pass ([Category and tags](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859673?hl=en); [Verify your website](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13205715?hl=en)). Custom store listings are capped at 50 per app, and store listing experiments stop automatically after six months ([Custom store listings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867158?hl=en); [Store listing experiments](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6227309?hl=en)).

## Google's APIs and tools expose almost nothing about review verdicts

### What the official APIs can and cannot read

The Google Play Developer API (Publishing API) needs a Google Cloud project, the API enabled, and a service account invited under Users and permissions in Play Console; linking the developer account to a Cloud project is no longer required ([Getting started](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/getting_started)). It works through edits: a copy of the deployed state where nothing changes until commit, with any Console change invalidating open edits. It can upload bundles, assign tracks, edit listings and images, manage testers, validate an edit (`edits.validate`), list release states, and upload the Data safety CSV. It cannot create an app, perform the first upload, or accept legal consents ([Edits](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/edits); [API reference](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest)). No resource exposes policy status, rejection reasons, App content declarations, App access details or pre-launch results, and the Data safety form cannot be read back. Track release `status` takes only `draft`, `inProgress`, `halted` and `completed` ([edits.tracks](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/edits.tracks)). Rejection reasons therefore arrive only by email and in the Console; the researchers found no Pub/Sub or webhook for review state, which is an inferred absence and not a Google statement.

Adjacent APIs fill narrow roles. The Play Developer Reporting API exposes Android vitals such as crash and ANR rates ([Play Developer Reporting API](https://developers.google.com/play/developer/reporting)). The Reviews API returns production reviews from the last week with quotas of 200 GET requests per hour and 2,000 POST per day ([Reply to Reviews API](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/reply-to-reviews)). Developer verification has its own status and registration APIs usable from CI ([Verification FAQ](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq)). Only a fast subset of pre-review checks is available through the API; most run only on the Console website, and the subset is undocumented ([Pre-review checks](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14807773)).

### Third-party tools supply building blocks but no cross-artifact view

Two MIT-licensed command-line tools are the closest prior art. **GPC `preflight`** (TypeScript) runs nine scanners offline on an AAB: manifest flags (targetSdk, debuggable, testOnly, cleartext, missing `android:exported` and `foregroundServiceType`), 18 restricted permissions, 64-bit and 16 KB native-library checks, listing metadata from a fastlane directory, embedded secrets, non-Play billing SDKs, tracking SDKs and Play Billing Library deadlines, with JSON output and a non-zero exit code; it treats Data safety only as reminders and may skip manifest scanners on large bundles ([GPC preflight docs](https://yasserstudio.github.io/gpc/commands/preflight)). **Greenlight** (Go) scans source or binaries for target API, billing library, restricted permissions, foreground-service types, account-deletion paths and 16 KB, with SARIF and JUnit output; its own documentation notes that source scans see only the pre-merge manifest and that static analysis proves a flow exists in code and not that it works ([Greenlight](https://github.com/RevylAI/greenlight)). Older security tools remain useful as components: MobSF (GPL-3.0) performs static analysis of AABs and dynamic analysis with traffic capture, and Exodus (AGPL-3.0) detects tracker SDKs by class signature in an APK ([MobSF](https://github.com/MobSF/Mobile-Security-Framework-MobSF); [exodus-standalone](https://github.com/Exodus-Privacy/exodus-standalone)). fastlane supply offers a validate-only mode against Play and defines the de facto metadata folder layout, and a GitHub Action validates that folder statically ([fastlane supply](https://docs.fastlane.tools/actions/supply/); [validate-fastlane-supply-metadata](https://github.com/ashutoshgngwr/validate-fastlane-supply-metadata)).

The rest of the market is listing-only web checkers and LLM wrappers (SubmitSafe, AppDrift, Policy Lint, several agent skills) plus one enterprise module, Appknox Store Release Readiness, whose check list is unpublished ([SubmitSafe listing](https://www.stork.ai/en/submitsafe-pre-check-for-google-play); [AppDrift](https://appdrift.co/rejection-scanner); [Appknox](https://www.appknox.com/press-release/appknox-adds-portfolio-wide-app-store-release-readiness-to-its-mobile-security-platform)). None has published accuracy data, no independent benchmark against real rejections exists, and none ingests the Data safety form or Console declarations for cross-checking.

## A pre-submission checker should diff artifacts against each other

### Design principles that follow from how rejections happen

Almost every common rejection is a mismatch between two artifacts, and third-party SDKs are the hidden cause behind Data safety, permission, foreground-service, payments, SDK-version and 16 KB flags because manifest merging and bundled native libraries change the app without the developer writing a line. A checker built on cross-artifact consistency with an SDK inventory at its centre will therefore cover more real rejections than one built on single-artifact rules. Four further principles come straight from the loop mechanics. Run every check regardless of earlier failures, because the point is to surface in one pass what Google surfaces serially. Scan every active version code on every track and not just the release candidate. Name each finding with Google's literal policy name, mapping legacy and current names (Minimum Functionality and Limited Functionality and Content) to one rule, so findings match future emails. Emit two severities, blocker for policy and requirement failures and eligibility for recommended items, and make every rule date-aware so that 2027 rules warn before they block.

The researchers' recommended input contract has seven parts: the release AAB; a fastlane or Gradle Play Publisher style metadata folder with listing text and images per locale; the Data safety CSV export; the privacy-policy and account-deletion URLs; the App access credentials and instructions; an optional service-account JSON used read-only to enumerate tracks; and a short self-attestation questionnaire for the App content declarations that no API exposes.

### Checks that need a device or emulator run

Static analysis has a known ceiling: a Delete Account button wired to nothing passes it ([Greenlight](https://github.com/RevylAI/greenlight)). The device stage should install the split APKs that bundletool generates from the bundle, or the Play-signed build from internal app sharing, on a clean device, because reviewers install the Google-signed release build and signing-key mismatches with API providers are a reported cause of Broken Functionality flags (vendor source) ([TesterBee](https://testerbee.com/blog/google-play-broken-functionality-rejection-checks)). The run should then confirm that the app cold-starts, loads and responds under a monkey or Robo crawl; that a scripted login succeeds with the exact App access credentials from a non-local network with no OTP prompt and that paywalled areas are reachable; that any prominent disclosure appears before the runtime permission dialog and that dismissing it is not treated as consent; that the in-app privacy-policy link and the account-deletion path exist and lead somewhere; that no interstitial fires on launch or exit and that every full-screen ad shows a working close control within 15 seconds (5 for child audiences); that a licence-tester purchase is acknowledged and not auto-refunded after the three-minute test window, and that entitlement restores after reinstall ([Test your Play Billing integration](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/test)); and that the layout survives edge-to-edge, predictive back and a tablet-sized window at targetSdk 36. Capturing network traffic from the release build and diffing it against the Data safety CSV confirms real data flows, a technique QAwerk recommends and MobSF supports ([QAwerk](https://qawerk.com/blog/google-play-rejection-reasons/)). A run on a 16 KB emulator image closes the native-code check.

### Checks that need human or LLM judgement

Judgement is unavoidable where the policy has no threshold. This covers impersonation and intellectual property in names, icons and screenshots; whether each claim in the listing is demonstrable in the build; whether an app is a bare WebView or has limited functionality (a heuristic of single activity, WebView class use and a tiny dex can pre-screen); whether the developer's own apps are too similar to each other, which a tool can score across four sibling apps even though Google publishes no similarity threshold; target-audience and content-rating fit; restricted content in the app and its ads; whether a subscription offer screen carries every term, which an LLM can assess from a captured screenshot; whether the privacy-policy text covers the data types the scanners detected; whether a sensitive permission's core use case qualifies and whether the declaration video shows it end to end; whether a detected payment SDK sells physical or digital goods; and the quality of the production-access questionnaire answers. LLM output here should be presented as risk flags with the policy citation and never as a verdict, since no tool has published precision or recall against real rejections.

Two inputs cannot be fetched at all. App content declarations are not readable through any API, so the checker must rely on attestation or user-supplied exports, and opt-in tester counts for the 12-tester gate are not known to be API-readable. Scraping the Play Console web UI to obtain them is not addressed in the API documentation but sits badly with Google's general terms on automated access, so user-supplied exports, a pasted rejection email mapped to the rule catalogue, and self-attestation are the compliant substitutes ([Google Terms of Service](https://policies.google.com/terms?hl=en)).

### What to reuse, what to build, and the terms that constrain the design

GPC and Greenlight can be studied or wrapped for the manifest, permission, native-library, secrets and billing checks. The Exodus tracker-signature database is the standard SDK fingerprint dataset, with AGPL obligations on the code and a data licence the researchers did not check. MobSF's REST API can supply manifest, permission, secret and tracker JSON if a GPL service boundary is acceptable. The fastlane metadata layout is the interchange format for listing text and images. Google's own insights-lint library and the `PlaySdkIndexNonCompliant` lint check should run in CI alongside the checker, since each covers a slice the others miss.

Six capabilities are unoccupied ground and form the checker's real value: a Data safety CSV diff against detected SDKs and permissions; an all-tracks active-bundle audit; an App access credential liveness test; privacy-policy content analysis against detected data types; listing-versus-build claim comparison; and a rule catalogue keyed to the literal policy names in rejection emails. The main dataset to build is the SDK-to-data-type map, sourced from each SDK vendor's published Data safety guidance linked from the SDK Index; no public machine-readable feed of SDK Index statuses was identified.

The Play Developer API terms prohibit offering a tool that creates, uploads, publishes or updates apps on behalf of third parties and prohibit sharing account passwords or service-account keys ([API usage instructions](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api_usage); [API terms](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/terms)). The lower-risk architecture, in the researchers' reading, is a read-only analyser that runs locally or in the developer's own CI with the developer's own service account; a hosted service that holds customers' keys is the risky design, and whether read-only delegated access counts as third-party access is a legal question no source answered.

## What this reference cannot guarantee

**Wording is paraphrased.** Every researcher read Google's pages through a fetch tool that summarises long pages; the Developer Program Policy page is about 294,000 characters and was read in chunks, and several Help pages were truncated. Rule statements here are close paraphrase with exact numbers as read. Anything destined for an appeal or a legal document should be copied from the live page.

**Dates conflict in five places.** The 16 KB block date is 1 February 2027 on the current developer page against an original 1 November 2025 announcement and third-party claims of enforcement since November 2025. Contacts and location changes show 27 January 2027 on Google's live table against 28 October 2026 in a secondary article. Japan's fee-model date is 30 September 2026 in the Help Center against December 2026 in Google's March blog. US external-link download reporting is due 1 February 2027 on Google's page against 1 December 2026 in a third-party blog. D-U-N-S lead time is 30 days on one Google page and 28 on another. In each case this report follows the live Google page, and the existing-app target API thresholds for TV, XR and Automotive follow developer.android.com over an inconsistent Help Center summary.

**Several features and rules are unconfirmed.** Whether parallel publishing, the Submission history log and AI recommendations had shipped by November 2026 was not verified, and no reference documentation was found for the release status API. The Console advertising ID declaration is described only by third-party guides. Upload blocks for debuggable builds, debug signing and `com.example` packages rest on CI-vendor documentation. Whether outdated (non-critical) SDK versions block releases is unresolved. The Checks product's current availability and pricing could not be confirmed. Developer-name and release-name length limits, a per-screenshot file-size limit, the number of experiment variants (two as fetched, three in older documentation), and the news freshness standard of 30 days were not confirmed on a fully read official page. The exact `bundletool dump manifest`, `apkanalyzer` and `aapt2` command syntax comes from background knowledge and was not re-verified.

**Some evidence is anecdotal.** The explanation for one-flag-per-round reviews, the staged-pipeline model, the wording of rejection notices and the frequency ranking of rejection reasons come from forum reports, vendor blogs and the researchers' synthesis; several Reddit threads were read only as search snippets. The transparency-report statistics come from a single vendor relay. Google publishes no per-policy rejection counts, no suspension threshold for repeated rejections, no appeal turnaround, no definition of sufficient tester engagement, no thresholds for limited functionality, and no description of how it detects Data safety discrepancies.

**Coverage has edges.** The list of countries where gambling apps are eligible, opt-out price-increase countries, Teacher Approved criteria, pre-registration mechanics beyond the limits cited, version-code rules beyond the maximum, and any policy announcement after 29 October 2026 were not researched. Legal questions, including the Developer Distribution Agreement and whether a hosted checker counts as third-party API access, were not reviewed.

## Conclusion

The review loop is best understood as a consistency test run by someone who stops at the first inconsistency. Google's 2026 direction confirms this reading: it is moving failure detection earlier, into the IDE, CI and Console pre-checks, and isolating collateral delay through queue protection and announced per-track reviews, without promising faster human review. A developer's fastest path is therefore to perform Google's consistency test first, completely, and across every track, and then to leave the submission untouched until it clears. For four apps on one account this is also risk management, because repeated rejections accumulate toward suspension at the account level.

For the checker, the strategic insight is that the valuable half of the problem is the half existing tools ignore. Manifest and native-library rules are already solved in open source and are the part Google blocks deterministically at upload anyway. The rejections that cost a week each come from artifacts that only the developer holds together: the Data safety CSV, the reviewer credentials, the listing claims, the privacy policy and the old bundles still active on test tracks. A tool that joins those, keeps its rules dated against Google's quarterly announcements, and reports in Google's own policy vocabulary would occupy ground that neither Google's fragmented first-party tools nor the current crop of LLM wrappers covers. Its limits should be stated as plainly as Google states its own: passing every check does not guarantee approval, because the final judgement on functionality, deception and audience fit remains a human one.
