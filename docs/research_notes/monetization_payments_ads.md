# Google Play Monetization Policies: Payments, In-App Purchases, Subscriptions and Ads (state as of November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-14. All official pages were fetched on that date; unless a page prints its own date, "as of Nov 2026" means "as the live page read on 2026-11-14". Page content was read through a summarising fetch tool, so wording is paraphrased unless in quotation marks; a few fee tables were flattened in extraction and are flagged where column mapping is uncertain.

## 1. Payments policy: when Google Play's billing system is mandatory, exemptions, anti-steering, and how it changed after Epic v. Google

### Takeaway
The global default is unchanged: paid downloads and in-app digital goods/services must use Google Play's billing system (GPB) and apps may not steer users to other payment methods, except under Payments policy Section 3 (physical goods and other excluded categories), Section 8 (alternative billing programs) and Section 9 (external offers/links programs). The big change is regional: in the US Google stopped requiring GPB and stopped anti-steering enforcement from October 29, 2025 (Epic injunction), and from June 30, 2026 a "billing choice program" plus a new split fee model started in the EEA/UK (US via its own programs), with Australia/Japan on September 30, 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Core rules (global, as of Nov 2026)**
- Rule 1: Developers charging for app downloads from Google Play "must use Google Play's billing system as the method of payment". — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)
- Rule 2: Play-distributed apps that require or accept payment for in-app features or services (items, subscriptions, ad-free versions, cloud services, app functionality/content) must use GPB unless Section 3, 8 or 9 applies. — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)
- Rule 3 (GPB must NOT be used): payments primarily for physical goods or physical services, credit-card or utility bill remittances; peer-to-peer payments, online auctions and tax-exempt donations; payments for content/services that facilitate online gambling (per the Real-Money Gambling policy); and any product category unacceptable under Google's Payments Center Content Policies. — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en); [Payments Center Content Policies](https://support.google.com/payments/merchant/answer/75724)
- Rule 4 (anti-steering): other than under Sections 3, 8 and 9, apps may not lead users to a payment method other than GPB. This covers the store listing, in-app promotions, webviews, buttons, links, messaging, ads, and sign-up/account-creation flows. — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)
- Rule 5: In-app virtual currencies may only be used within the app or game title where they were purchased. — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)
- Rule 6: Pricing and terms must be clear and accurate; in-app pricing must match what the Play billing UI shows; the store listing must disclose when features require an extra charge. — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)
- Rule 7 (loot boxes): apps offering mechanisms to receive randomised virtual items from a purchase must clearly disclose the odds of receiving those items in advance of, and in close and timely proximity to, the purchase. — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)
- Rule 8: Developers in eligible countries/regions may offer an alternative billing system inside the app after enrolling in the applicable program and accepting its terms. — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en); [Alternative billing topic](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/topic/16471708)
- Rule 9: Developers in eligible countries/regions may lead users to offers outside the app, subject to program requirements and enrollment. — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en); [External offers topic](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/topic/16470499)

**Digital vs physical (examples Google gives; no formal definition)**
- Digital, GPB required: virtual currencies, extra lives/playtime, add-on items, characters, avatars; subscriptions (fitness, game, dating, education, music, video, other content); app functionality or content (ad-free version, features not in the free version); cloud software and services (storage, productivity, financial management software). — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Physical, GPB not supported: groceries, clothing, appliances, electronics; transport, airfare, gym memberships, food delivery; credit-card or utility bill payments. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Mixed SKUs: GPB is required where the SKU has more digital than physical content, or is marketed as digital. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)

**Situations where GPB is not required (global)**
- Consumption-only ("reader") apps where nothing, digital or physical, can be bought in the app are allowed, even if content belongs to a paid service bought elsewhere; users may log in to access it. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Goods consumed outside the app (e.g. device ringtones not usable in the app, web-only content, cloud-management apps that give no in-app storage access). — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Gift cards: in-app sale of eGift or physical gift cards is exempt from GPB. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Loyalty/reward points may be earned and redeemed in-app; selling points or any virtual currency in-app requires GPB. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Regulated clinical services from licensed healthcare providers; insurance, stock trades, investment consulting, tax preparation and filing: not billed through Play. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- 1:1 online paid services between two individuals that are not replayable afterwards (music/art lessons, personal training, counselling, specialist advice; excludes customer support) are exempt. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Tips/contributions are exempt only if 100% goes to the creator and the payment grants no digital content or perks (stickers, badges, emojis). — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Telecom/broadband/satellite/cable/IPTV providers may bill certain digital services to the customer's physical service bill under conditions (separate eligibility form). — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Countries where Play billing is not available: the GPB requirement does not apply there. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- AI/agentic experiences: the Payments policy applies if a Play-managed app facilitates digital purchases, including inside chat agents or embedded third-party experiences. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- App category does not change the billing requirement; Google's own apps must follow the same policy; price parity across platforms is not required; developers may refund and support users directly; distribution via other stores/websites needs no GPB. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)

**Anti-steering detail (outside the exception regions)**
- Not allowed in the app: direct links to a page that could lead to a non-Play payment method, or language encouraging purchase outside the app. Allowed: a consumption-only app telling users about purchase options without a direct link (Google's example: "Need extra lives? Head to our website to purchase more."); links to administrative pages (account management, privacy policy, help centre) that do not lead to a prohibited payment method; contacting users by email or other out-of-app channels about alternative purchase options and pricing. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- App review access: if purchase/content is gated behind login, provide credentials via App Access in Play Console, or risk blocked updates or removal. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)

**United States: Epic v. Google injunction and settlement (mobile and tablet only)**
- Google's page states "On September 12, 2025, the Ninth Circuit upheld changes to Android and Google Play", and that "to ensure compliance with the injunction as of October 29, 2025" Google stopped prohibiting developers from communicating with US users about pricing/availability outside Play, from linking to outside downloads or transactions, and from using payment methods other than GPB, and stopped requiring GPB for US users. — [Policy changes for developers serving US users](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en)
- December 9, 2025: launch of the US payments policy update, the US alternative billing program and the US external content links program. — [US changes page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en)
- March 4, 2026: Google entered a new settlement with Epic and "the parties have asked the US District Court to enter a revised Modified Injunction"; current policies "remain in effect" pending that. Google's blog the same day says it "resolved our disputes worldwide with Epic Games". — [US changes page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en); [Android Developers Blog, 4 Mar 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/a-new-era-for-choice-and-openness.html)
- July 22, 2026: developers enrolled in the US external content links and alternative billing programs were told they must "report transactions and successful downloads and pay the relevant service fees starting on October 1, 2026". October 21, 2026 update: "developers enrolled in external content links have until February 1, 2027 to report successful downloads" and pay those fees. — [US changes page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en)
- Also US-only: Play Catalog Access program and Third-party App Store on Play program; developers had to opt out by July 22, 2026 or their listings are shared with participating third-party US Android stores (programs effective July 22, 2026; third-party store onboarding from July 15, 2026). — [US changes page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en)
- US alternative billing program: opt-in; mobile/tablet apps and games for users in the US and territories; alternative billing may be offered "in lieu of or alongside Google Play Billing"; if GPB is also used it must be accessible to all users "in a consistent and reliable manner". Requirements: PCI-DSS if handling cards, comply with law incl. protection of minors, customer support and an unauthorised-transaction dispute process, links for order history/subscription management/refund requests, subscription management URLs in Play Console, integrate alternative billing APIs, report all authorised US transactions within 24 hours (once required), pay service fees. Google may modify or end the program if the court order changes. — [Offering an alternative billing system for users in the United States](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16497028?hl=en)
- US alternative billing service fees (new/existing install cutoff June 30, 2026): first US$1M annual earnings 10%; auto-renewing subscriptions 10%; other digital items 20% (new installs) / 25% (existing installs); Level Up / Apps Experience participants 15% / 20%. (Table was flattened in extraction; mapping inferred.) — [US alternative billing page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16497028?hl=en)
- US external content links program: optional, mobile/tablet apps and games, links limited to US users; can be combined with GPB and/or alternative billing; no cap on number of links. Requirements: enroll and get approval for the app and any linked external apps; integrate the external content links APIs (surface an information screen, enable parental controls, support reporting); customer support, dispute process and refunds (unless clearly non-refundable before purchase); tell users the destination and purpose before linking out; no unsecured PII in URLs; no misleading redirects. Download destinations must show app name, icon, description, version, size, developer info, install only on explicit user action, have public support and data policies; external apps must be registered in Play Console (review up to 7 days or longer) and each update re-registered. — [Enrolling in the external content links program (US)](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16470497?hl=en)
- US external content links fees: apply to transactions/installs completed within 24 hours of following a link. Purchases: 10% first US$1M, 10% subscriptions, 20% other (15% for Level Up/Apps Experience). App download events: fixed fee per install "subject to periodic adjustments" of US$3.65 (games) and US$2.85 (apps). If the link leads to an install, only the install fee applies to that link-out. Reporting from October 1, 2026 must include all transactions incl. $0 free trials, renewals, top-ups, upgrades/downgrades; download reporting/payment deadline February 1, 2027. — [Enrolling in the external content links program (US)](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16470497?hl=en)
- Secondary reporting says the US injunction terms run until November 1, 2027. — [stash.gg analysis (secondary, 2026)](https://www.stash.gg/blog/googles-october-1-external-link-fees-what-changes-and-what-developers-still-dont-know)

**Billing choice program (UK, EEA from June 30, 2026; Australia, Japan from September 30, 2026)**
- Opt-in program for mobile/tablet apps and games selling digital content/services: developer may integrate its own in-app billing (alternative billing) or link users to its website or a pre-installed payment app (external web links). GPB must be offered alongside; users must get a choice. US: no billing choice program; existing US alternative billing and external content links programs continue. EEA: user choice billing, the EEA standalone alternative billing program and the external offers program remain available as well. — [Enrolling in the billing choice program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17161464?hl=en)
- Requirements: PCI-DSS where applicable; legal compliance incl. minors; customer support and dispute process; refunds unless clearly disclosed non-refundable; links for order history, subscription management and refunds; use Google's rendered choice screen or your own built to Google's UX guidelines (declare in Play Console); set external web link preferences in Play Console before linking out; integrate billing choice APIs (one-time information screen, parental controls, reporting); report authorised transactions within 24 hours; submit enrollment changes in Console before app updates. — [Enrolling in the billing choice program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17161464?hl=en)
- External web link rules: link only to offers you own; state destination and purpose before link-out; no unsecured PII; no misleading redirects; pre-installed payment apps only for the specific item the user selected. Fees apply to purchases completed within 24 hours of following a link. — [Enrolling in the billing choice program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17161464?hl=en)
- Announcement: Google's 24 June 2026 blog says billing choice is available to all developers globally serving users in the UK and EEA, plus existing US programs, expanding to more markets later. — [Android Developers Blog, 24 Jun 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/play-expanded-billing.html)
- Billing choice APIs shipped in Play Billing Library 9.1.0 (released 2026-06-18): `getBillingChoiceInfoAsync()`, `showBillingProgramInformationDialog()`, `ChoiceScreenType`. — [PBL release notes](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/release-notes)

**EEA (Digital Markets Act)**
- EEA options: (a) user choice billing on any form factor; (b) EEA alternative billing without user choice (standalone, no GPB needed); (c) external offers program; (d) billing choice program from June 30, 2026. — [Understanding user choice billing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13821247?hl=en); [Billing choice program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17161464?hl=en)
- EEA external offers program: app must offer external offers to EEA users (any form factor), must not target only children, developer must be registered as a business. Enrolled apps may use only alternative billing without user choice for in-app purchases (GPB and user choice billing not allowed in the same app). Must integrate external offers APIs (required information screen), report transactions within 24 hours, provide support/refunds/dispute handling, keep store listing free of information about off-Play apps or purchases, and may link to own offers and app downloads. — [Enroll your app in the external offers program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14372887?hl=en)
- EEA external offers fees effective June 4, 2026 (transactions/installs within 24 hours of a link): initial acquisition fee 0%; ongoing service fee 10% auto-renewing subscriptions, 20% other in-app digital items (15% in the new Apps & Games programs; 10% on first US$1M annual earnings); app download €1.20 per install (subject to periodic adjustment). Key dates: Dec 9, 2025 updated APIs and download links; Jan 28, 2026 deadline for pre-December-2025 enrollees to adopt updated requirements/APIs. — [Enroll your app in the external offers program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14372887?hl=en)

**India and South Korea**
- Developers may offer an alternative billing system alongside GPB for in-app purchases on phones and tablets (gaming and non-gaming); service fee is reduced by 4 percentage points on alternative-billing transactions (e.g. 15% becomes 11%). South Korea APIs mandatory since Aug 2, 2023; India manual-reporting sunset date "TBA". — [Understanding user choice billing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13821247?hl=en); [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en); [Service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)
- The new fee model reaches South Korea (with Turkey and South Africa) on December 31, 2026 and "Rest of World" (which would include India and Pakistan) on September 30, 2027. — [Understanding Google Play's lower service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16954621?hl=en)

**Japan (Mobile Software Competition Act, in force for designated companies December 17/18, 2025)**
- Google extended user choice billing in Japan from non-gaming apps (since 2022) to all apps and games selling digital content, and launched a program letting developers offer a choice between GPB and paying on the developer's website. — [Google blog: Complying with Japan's MSCA](https://blog.google/around-the-globe/google-asia/complying-with-mobile-software-competition-act/)
- Japan "external payments program": links must be offered side-by-side with GPB through the external payments API; cannot be combined with user choice billing in the same app; app must not target only under-13s; developer registered as business; store listing must not mention out-of-app purchases; link only to a payment page for the specific item the user selected. Fees (within 24 hours of link): 10% subscriptions, 10% first US$1M, 20% other. The program stops new enrollments September 30, 2026 and is discontinued after March 31, 2027; enrolled developers must move to the billing choice program by March 31, 2027. — [Enrolling in the external payments program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16787536?hl=en)

**United Kingdom**
- From June 30, 2026 UK-serving developers get lower service fees and can use their own billing alongside GPB or direct users to their websites; from September 30, 2026 the revamped Games Level Up and Apps Experience programs; a Registered App Stores program later in 2026. Google frames this as addressing UK CMA concerns. — [Google UK blog, 25 Jun 2026](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/around-the-globe/google-europe/united-kingdom/google-play-updates-uk-developers/)

**Other user choice billing pilot markets**
- User choice billing pilot markets: Australia, Brazil, Indonesia, Japan, South Africa, UK and the EEA. Outside EEA/Japan the pilot covers non-gaming mobile/tablet apps only. Brazil/Indonesia/South Africa: 4% fee reduction on alternative-billing transactions until updated fees roll out. Alternative billing APIs mandatory since March 13, 2024. — [Understanding user choice billing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13821247?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Pre-submission check (global default, incl. Pakistan/India-distributed apps until local programs apply): "App sells digital goods/features/subscriptions to users -> must use GPB unless (A) the item is in the Section 3 excluded list (physical goods/services, bill remittance, P2P, auctions, tax-exempt donations, licensed gambling), (B) the user is in a region where the developer is enrolled in an alternative billing/billing choice program, or (C) the developer is enrolled in an external offers/links program for that user's region."
- Check: "App has any link, button, webview or text that leads to non-Play payment for digital goods -> allowed only for users in US (enrolled or per injunction), EEA, UK, Japan, Australia under the relevant program; must be geo-gated to those users; otherwise reject risk under Rule 4."
- Check: "App sells loot boxes/gacha -> odds disclosed before and near the purchase."
- Check: "App sells virtual currency -> usable only in that same app/title."
- Check: "Tip jar -> exempt from GPB only if creator gets 100% and tipper gets no digital perk; otherwise GPB."
- Because enrollment in the US external content links and alternative billing programs is still described as required to use those options, a developer should enroll rather than simply add links, even though the injunction bars Google from prohibiting them; the official page does not spell out consequences of linking without enrollment.
- The Payments policy main page deliberately no longer names countries; the regional program pages are the authoritative source and change frequently, so each must be re-checked at submission time.

### Gaps
- The exact text of the revised Modified Injunction and whether the court has approved the March 2026 Epic settlement were not confirmed from a primary court source; the official page still describes it as pending. The November 1, 2027 expiry comes only from secondary coverage.
- A third-party blog reported a December 1, 2026 deadline for US download reporting; Google's page (October 21, 2026 update) says February 1, 2027. Treat the official date as current.
- Detailed India and South Korea program pages (answer/13306652 and answer/11222040) were not opened; only the summary rules above were confirmed.
- I found no official page describing a UK-specific or Australia-specific program other than the billing choice program.
- Official page on tax-exempt donation requirements (e.g. required registration of the non-profit) was not retrieved.

## 2. Service fee structure and 2025-2026 fee model changes

### Takeaway
There are now two fee regimes. In the "rollout" markets (EEA, UK, US from June 30, 2026; Australia, Japan from September 30, 2026) Google charges a service fee (10% on the first US$1M a year and on subscriptions; 20% new installs / 25% existing installs on other transactions) plus a separate 5% billing fee only when GPB processes the payment. Everywhere else, including South Asia, the legacy model applies until the global rollout (Korea/Turkey/South Africa December 31, 2026; rest of world September 30, 2027): 15% on the first US$1M, 30% above, 15% on auto-renewing subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
- Service fee is charged on all transactions whether via GPB, alternative billing or external web links; the billing fee is additional and only for GPB transactions, currently 5% in launched markets; billing fee for other regions "forthcoming". — [Understanding Google Play's lower service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16954621?hl=en)
- New-install table (rollout markets): Standard 20% non-recurring, 10% recurring, 20% paid app initial purchase; Apps & Games programs 15% / 10% / 15%; first US$1M annual earnings 10% across all; each "+ billing fee if applicable". — [Understanding Google Play's lower service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16954621?hl=en)
- Existing-install table: Standard 25% non-recurring in-app (GPB or alternative billing), 20% non-recurring via external web link, 10% recurring, 25% paid app; programs 20% / 15% / 10% / 20%; first US$1M 10%. (Tables were flattened in extraction; verify cells on the live page.) — [Understanding Google Play's lower service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16954621?hl=en)
- "New install" = the user's first install from Play (or first update from Play if not originally installed from Play) occurred on or after the regional rollout date; otherwise "existing install". The rollout date for each region is the threshold. — [Understanding Google Play's lower service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16954621?hl=en)
- Rollout timeline: June 30, 2026 EEA, UK, US; September 30, 2026 AU, JP (and new Apps & Games program rate cards in AU, EEA, JP, UK, US); December 31, 2026 KR, TR, ZA; September 30, 2027 Rest of World. — [Understanding Google Play's lower service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16954621?hl=en)
- The 5% billing fee applies when a user buys an in-app digital item via GPB or makes the initial purchase of a paid app/game. Google says 97% of developers pay no fee. — [Service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)
- All other markets, until the announced updated fees roll out globally: 15% on the first US$1M of annual developer revenue for developers enrolled in the 15% tier, 30% above US$1M; 15% for automatically renewing subscriptions regardless of revenue; 15% or lower for eligible programs such as Play Media Experience Program. — [Service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)
- The 15% tier is not automatic: needs a payments profile, creation of an Account Group declaring all Associated Developer Accounts, and acceptance of the 15% tier terms in Play Console; threshold is per calendar year across the whole account group; applied from the date enrollment completes. — [Changes to Google Play's service fee in 2021](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10632485?hl=en)
- Games Level Up and Apps Experience: participants get 15% (new installs) / 20% (existing installs) on non-recurring SKUs, excluding billing fee; enrollment open from September 1, 2026 and rate cards available September 30, 2026 in AU, EEA, JP, UK, US. No change yet to Play Media Experience Program. — [Understanding Google Play's lower service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16954621?hl=en); [Billing choice program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17161464?hl=en); [Android Developers Blog, 24 Jun 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/play-expanded-billing.html)
- Announcement history: 4 March 2026 blog (fees: 5% billing fee in EEA/UK/US, IAP service fee 20% for new installs, 10% recurring subscriptions; rollout "by June 30" EEA/UK/US, "by September 30" Australia, "by December 31" Korea and Japan, "by September 30, 2027" rest of world); 24 June 2026 blog confirmed start on June 30, 2026 and the 10% first-US$1M service fee. — [Android Developers Blog, 4 Mar 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/a-new-era-for-choice-and-openness.html); [Android Developers Blog, 24 Jun 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/play-expanded-billing.html)
- Conflict: the March 2026 blog put Japan in the December 31, 2026 group; the current Help Center timeline puts Japan with Australia on September 30, 2026 and lists KR, TR, ZA for December 31, 2026. — [March blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/a-new-era-for-choice-and-openness.html); contradicted by [lower service fees page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16954621?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Effective all-in cost with GPB in rollout markets: 15% (10% + 5%) on first US$1M and on subscriptions; 25% (new installs) or 30% (existing installs) on other transactions above US$1M. So a small developer using GPB sees roughly the same 15% as before; the saving comes from using alternative billing or web links (10%) minus own payment-processing cost.
- A developer in Pakistan selling to US/EEA/UK users is charged by the buyer's region rule ("Users in Australia, EEA, Japan, UK, or US"), so the new model already applies to those sales; sales to users in Pakistan/India stay on the legacy 15%/30% model until September 30, 2027 (this follows from the fee page's "users in" wording, but is my reading, not an explicit statement).
- Check: "Developer account sells digital goods -> confirm enrollment in 15% tier (account group declared) or it may be charged 30% in legacy markets."

### Gaps
- Whether the first-US$1M 10% tier in rollout markets requires the same account-group enrollment as the legacy 15% tier is not stated on the pages fetched.
- Billing fee percentage for markets other than EEA/UK/US (and presumably AU/JP) is unannounced ("forthcoming").
- Program requirements for Apps Experience / Games Level Up were not researched (non-monetisation scope).

## 3. Subscriptions policy: disclosures, trials, cancellation, price changes, rejection reasons

### Takeaway
A subscription offer screen must itself show, without any extra tap, the price, billing period, auto-renewal, what is included, and whether a subscription is needed to use the app; trials must state duration, conversion price and how to cancel; the app must contain an easy online way to cancel/manage. A new disclosure rule for eligibility-based discounts (student plans etc.) was announced October 29, 2026 with a January 27, 2027 deadline.

### Cited Findings
- Do not mislead users about subscription services or content in any in-app promotion, splash screen or plan-selection screen. Offer must clearly disclose terms, cost, billing frequency, auto-renewal terms, whether a subscription is required to use the app, and other material information; "Users should not have to perform any additional action to review the information." — [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)
- Subscriptions must provide sustained or recurring value throughout their life; they may not be used for effectively one-time benefits (e.g. a lump sum of in-app currency or single-use boosters) - those must be one-time products. — [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)
- Free trials and introductory offers: before enrollment, clearly and accurately state duration, pricing, what content/services are accessible, how and when the offer converts to paid, what it will cost, and that the user can cancel before conversion; show how content can be accessed without the trial where applicable; pricing and terms must be localised. — [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)
- Management and cancellation: app must clearly disclose how to manage or cancel, and must include in-app access to an easy-to-use online cancellation method, in account settings or equivalent: a link to Google Play's Subscription Center and/or direct access to the developer's own cancellation process. — [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)
- Cancelling does not refund the current period by default; the user keeps access until period end (some countries' law gives immediate cancellation with prorated refund). Developers may offer more flexible refunds and must notify users of changes to subscription, cancellation and refund policies. — [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)
- Listed violation examples: auto-renewing plan not clearly stating it renews and charges each period; annual plan that prominently shows a monthly price; incompletely localised price/terms; SKU names such as "Free Trial" for products with automatic recurring charges; purchase flow where users can accidentally tap subscribe; subscriptions with no recurring value; requiring an auto-renewing subscription for a one-time benefit or cancelling a user's subscription without request; missing manage/cancel link in account settings. — [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)
- New rule (announced 2026-10-29, compliance 2027-01-27): subscriptions with ongoing eligibility-based discounts (e.g. student plans) must clearly disclose eligibility criteria, the verification process, and post-eligibility pricing and benefit changes. — [Policy deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780?hl=en); [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)
- Price decreases: automatic, no consent; user notified and charged lower price at next renewal. — [Understanding subscriptions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12154973?hl=en)
- Opt-in price increase (default): user must agree; otherwise subscription is cancelled automatically before the first charge at the higher price; at least 30 days' notification. — [Understanding subscriptions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12154973?hl=en)
- Opt-out price increase: only in certain countries/regions and for developers in good standing; notice minimum 30 or 60 days depending on country; maximum one per base plan per country in any 365 days; amount capped at the greater of 50% of current price or 17 US cents per day; developer must certify its ToS reserve the right to raise prices with notice; must show each affected user a prominent in-app notice at least 30 days before (name, old and new price, effective date, how to cancel). — [Understanding subscriptions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12154973?hl=en)
- Changing a base plan price puts existing subscribers into a legacy price cohort at their old price until the developer ends the cohort; offer-phase prices cannot be changed after purchase. — [Understanding subscriptions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12154973?hl=en)
- PBL 9.0.0 added in-app messaging for outstanding opt-in price increases (shown at most once every 7 days). — [PBL release notes](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/release-notes)
- PBL 8.1.0: suspended subscriptions (paused or payment declined) return `isSuspended() = true`; app must not grant access and should send the user to the subscriptions centre. — [PBL release notes](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/release-notes)

### Inferences
- Check: "Offer screen shows total price per billing period in the user's local currency, the period, 'auto-renews until cancelled', what is included, all on the same screen as the buy button, no 'see terms' tap needed."
- Check: "Annual plan -> annual total is the most prominent figure; per-month equivalent may only be secondary."
- Check: "Free trial -> trial length, date/price of first charge, cancel-anytime instruction on the offer screen; product ID/name not 'free'."
- Check: "Settings/account screen contains 'Manage subscription' deep link to `https://play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions` (optionally with sku and package) or own cancel flow."
- Check: "App with student/senior/regional-eligibility discount -> add eligibility, verification and post-eligibility price disclosure before January 27, 2027."

### Gaps

- Whether account-hold/grace-period settings are mandatory was not checked.

## 4. In-app purchase implementation requirements (Play Billing Library)

### Takeaway
As of November 2026, new apps and updates must ship Play Billing Library 8 or higher (PBL 7's deadline was August 31, 2026 and its extension ended November 1, 2026); the latest is 9.1.0. Every purchase must be verified, entitlement granted only when state is PURCHASED, and acknowledged or consumed within three days or Google refunds it automatically.

### Cited Findings
- PBL deprecation table (new app and update deadline / extension deadline): v6 Aug 31, 2025 / Nov 1, 2025; v7 Aug 31, 2026 / Nov 1, 2026; v8 Aug 31, 2027 / Nov 1, 2027; v9 Aug 31, 2028 / Nov 1, 2028. Each version has a two-year deprecation cycle. Existing unmaintained apps keep working, "but new apps and updates must use supported versions". Extension form is on the Policy status page in Play Console. — [PBL deprecation FAQ](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/deprecation-faq)
- Releases: 8.0.0 (2025-06-30), 8.1.0 (2025-11-06, minSdk 23), 8.2.0 (2025-12-09, external content links/external offers APIs), 8.3.0 (2025-12-23, external payments APIs), 9.0.0 (2026-05-19, targetSdk 35), 9.1.0 (2026-06-18, billing choice APIs). — [PBL release notes](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/release-notes)
- PBL 8 breaking changes: `querySkuDetailsAsync()` removed (use `queryProductDetailsAsync`), `queryPurchaseHistory()` removed, parameterless `enablePendingPurchases()` removed (use `enablePendingPurchases(PendingPurchasesParams.newBuilder().enableOneTimeProducts().build())`), `queryPurchasesAsync(skuType, ...)` overload removed, new `ProductDetailsResponseListener` signature, optional `enableAutoServiceReconnection()`. — [PBL release notes](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/release-notes)
- Purchase lifecycle: show products, launch flow, verify the purchase on your server, give content, then acknowledge (non-consumable/subscription) or consume (consumable). — [Integrate the Play Billing Library](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate)
- Acknowledgement "must be done within three days so that the purchase isn't automatically refunded and entitlement revoked". Only initial subscription purchases need acknowledging, renewals do not. Do not use the unacknowledged window as a way to reject purchases. — [Integrate the Play Billing Library](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate)
- Pending purchases: grant entitlement only when the state moves from PENDING to PURCHASED; tell the user a pending purchase must be completed. — [Integrate the Play Billing Library](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate)
- Restoring purchases: the app must call `queryPurchasesAsync()` (e.g. in `onResume`) to pick up purchases made while the app was not running, on another device, or outside the app. — [Integrate the Play Billing Library](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate)
- Always verify legitimacy before granting entitlement (backend verification with Play Developer API recommended); attach `obfuscatedAccountId`/`obfuscatedProfileId` to help fraud detection. — [Integrate the Play Billing Library](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate)
- Testing: license testers (configured in Play Console) can use sideloaded/debug builds with matching package name, get test payment methods ("Test instrument, always approves" / "always declines", slow test cards that approve or decline after a few minutes, a card that approves then charges back) and are not charged; taxes are not computed for test purchases. — [Test your Play Billing Library integration](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/test)
- "Users incur actual charges for their test track purchases unless the user is a license tester." Draft apps and internal-test-track apps are subject to daily spend limits; publish to closed/open/production for higher limits. — [Test your Play Billing Library integration](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/test)
- For license testers an unacknowledged purchase is refunded after 3 minutes (page also lists a 5-minute "purchase acknowledgement" test period for subscriptions). Test subscription renewals: 1 week/1 month -> 5 min, 3 months -> 10 min, 6 months -> 15 min, 1 year -> 30 min; max 6 renewals; free trial 3 min, grace period 5 min, account hold 10 min. — [Test your Play Billing Library integration](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/test)
- Play Billing Lab app lets license testers change Play country, replay trial/intro offers, test price changes, accelerate subscription states, and (with Google approval) test real payment methods; real-method test purchases must be refunded or they incur the service fee. — [Test your Play Billing Library integration](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/test)
- Test Gmail accounts "shouldn't be used to make purchases using real instruments on Google Play". — [Test your Play Billing Library integration](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/test)

### Inferences
- Check: "`com.android.billingclient:billing` >= 8.0.0 (ideally 9.x) in any build submitted after November 1, 2026; `com.google.android.play.billingclient.version` meta-data present in merged manifest."
- Check: "Every purchase path ends in acknowledge/consume; QA with a license tester confirms no auto-refund email after 3 minutes."
- Check: "Kill and reopen app after a slow-card purchase -> entitlement appears only after PURCHASED."

## 5. Google Play Ads policy

### Takeaway
Ads are treated as part of the app: they must match the content rating, be clearly ads, stay inside the app, be dismissible, and never appear unexpectedly as full-screen interstitials (e.g. at level start, before the splash/loading screen, or unclosable after 15 seconds). Lockscreen monetisation, ads outside the app, system-UI mimicry, ad fraud and "made for ads" interstitial spam are prohibited; child-directed ads must come only from Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs.

### Cited Findings
- Ad content: ads and associated offers are part of the app and must follow Restricted Content policies; gambling apps have extra ad requirements. — [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en)
- Inappropriate ads: ads must be appropriate for the app's content rating even if the ad content is otherwise policy-compliant (e.g. Teen-rated ad in an Everyone-rated app is a violation). — [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en)
- Deceptive ads: ads must not simulate or impersonate app UI, or OS notifications/warnings; it must be clear which app serves each ad. Violations: an app-icon-looking element that is an ad, ads styled as system notifications, a "feature" that only leads to ads. — [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en)
- Disruptive ads: cannot force users to click ads or submit personal information for advertising before using the app; ads must display only within the serving app and must not interfere with other apps, ads, the device, or system/device buttons and ports; ads that interfere with normal use must be "easily dismissible without penalty". Violations: full-screen ads with no dismiss, false dismiss buttons, ads placed where users tap for app functions, ads on the home screen after the user leaves the app, ads triggered by home button or exit. — [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en)
- Better Ads Experiences (not allowed): full-screen interstitials of any format (video, GIF, static) that show unexpectedly, typically when the user has chosen to do something else, e.g. at the start of a level or content segment; full-screen video interstitials before the app's loading/splash screen; full-screen interstitials of any format that are not closeable after 15 seconds. Opt-in interstitials or ones that do not interrupt actions (e.g. after a game's score screen) may exceed 15 seconds. Not covered: rewarded ads the user explicitly opts into, and monetisation that does not interfere with normal use (integrated video content, non-full-screen banners). — [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en)
- Made for ads: apps may not show interstitial ads repeatedly after consecutive user actions so as to distract users from in-app tasks. — [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en)
- Lockscreen monetisation: unless the exclusive purpose of the app is a lockscreen, apps may not introduce ads or features that monetise the locked display. — [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en)
- Ad fraud is strictly prohibited: ads must be visible and rendered in-app (no hidden/off-screen ads), clicks must be genuine user actions (no auto-clicking, bots), no ads when user is not in the app, no tricking into accidental clicks, no fake install attribution, and the app must identify itself accurately to ad networks (package name, platform, inventory). — [Ad Fraud policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9969955?hl=en)
- Location data for ads: must be disclosed and covered in the privacy policy; location permissions may not be requested solely for ads. — [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en)
- Android advertising ID (AAID): use only for advertising and user analytics; check the user's opt-out/personalisation setting on each access; do not link to persistent device identifiers (SSAID, MAC, IMEI) for advertising; link to PII only with explicit consent; after reset do not connect the new ID to the old one without explicit consent; respect opt-out (no profiling/personalisation, but contextual ads, frequency capping, conversion tracking, reporting and fraud detection allowed); disclose in privacy notice; use AAID instead of any other device identifier for advertising. — [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en)
- AD_ID permission: apps targeting Android 13 (API 33) or higher that use the advertising ID must declare `com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID` (ad SDKs such as play-services-ads already merge it in); when a user deletes their advertising ID, the API returns a string of zeros (all devices since April 1, 2022); App Set ID is recommended for non-ads uses. — [Advertising ID](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6048248?hl=en)
- "Contains ads" declaration: answered Yes/No under App content > Ads in Play Console. Counts as ads: banners/interstitials from ad SDKs (including ones promoting your own products), house ads, ad walls, native ads/sponsored content in feeds. Does not count: a "More apps" section in the main menu, paid product placement, or offers to make in-app purchases. Misstating it is a policy violation that can lead to suspension; Google can verify and apply the label itself. — [Prepare your app for review](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455?hl=en)

**Families ads and monetisation (apps targeting children or mixed audiences)**
- Ads shown to children or users of unknown age must come only from Google Play Families Self-Certified Ads SDK versions; no interest-based advertising or remarketing; ads must be child-appropriate and follow the Families ad format rules. — [Families Ads and Monetization](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)
- Prohibited formats for children: ads/ad walls that cannot be clearly dismissed; rewarded or opt-in ads not closeable after 5 seconds; interstitials shown immediately on app launch; multiple ad placements on one page (more than one banner or video); ads not clearly distinguishable from app content (offerwalls, immersive ads); shocking or emotionally manipulative tactics; dismiss buttons that trigger another ad; ads placed where the user taps for app functions. Ads that do not interfere with normal use may exceed 5 seconds. — [Families Ads and Monetization](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)
- Child-only apps must not transmit AAID, SIM serial, build serial, BSSID, MAC, SSID, IMEI or IMSI, and should not request AD_ID permission when targeting API 33+; mixed-audience apps must not transmit those from children or unknown-age users. — [Families Ads and Monetization](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)
- Monetisation rules for children: IAP offers, cross-promotion and product placement count as commercial content; overly aggressive commercial tactics can lead to rejection/removal; child-only apps must clearly distinguish virtual coins from real-money purchases. — [Families Ads and Monetization](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)
- Self-certified SDK list with minimum versions (as listed Nov 2026): AdColony 4.8.0, AddApptr AATKit-family-safe 3.0.3, Chartboost 9.1.1, DT Exchange 8.2.1, Google Ad Manager IMA 3.25.1 / PAL 18.0.0, Google AdMob play-services-ads 19.0.0, HyprMX 6.0.3, InMobi 10.5.5, Kidoz 8.9.4, SuperAwesome 8.4.3, Unity Ads 4.0.1, Vungle 6.10.4. AppLovin and ironSource did not appear on the fetched list. In-house ads and direct deals do not need certification. Mixed-audience apps may use other SDKs only for non-child users (e.g. behind a neutral age screen). — [Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9283445?hl=en)
- Gambling/real-money game ads inside any Play app: allowed only if lawful and licensed where shown, never shown to known under-18s, app is not in Designed for Families and does not target under-18s, responsible gambling info is shown, and the host app has no simulated gambling content or gambling support features. — [Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Check: "Interstitial fires on app launch, on exit/back-to-home, or at start of a level -> policy violation; move to natural breaks (after score screen, between content)."
- Check: "Every full-screen ad has a working close control within 15 seconds (5 seconds for child audiences), unless it is a user-opted rewarded ad."
- Check: "No ad view overlapping or adjacent to tappable UI; no ad rendered when app is backgrounded; no notification-style or lockscreen ads."
- Check: "Play Console 'Contains ads' = Yes whenever any ad SDK, house ad or native sponsored content exists."
- Check: "Target API 33+ and uses ad ID -> AD_ID permission in merged manifest; child-only app -> AD_ID permission removed (`tools:node=\"remove\"`)."

## 6. AdMob / Google Mobile Ads policies that intersect with Play rejections

### Takeaway
AdMob adds placement rules stricter than Play's: no interstitials on app load or exit (use app open ads on the splash screen instead), at most one interstitial per two user actions, no ads on screens without content or dead-end screens, no incentivised clicks, and a Google-certified TCF CMP for personalised ads in the EEA, UK and Switzerland. Violations lead to limited or disabled ad serving rather than Play removal, but the same implementations often breach Play's Disruptive Ads rules.

### Cited Findings
- AdMob publishers must follow AdSense program policies; Google may disable ad serving to an app and/or the AdMob account. — [AdMob policies and restrictions](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6128543?hl=en)
- Invalid activity: "Publishers may not click their own ads or use any means to inflate impressions and/or clicks artificially"; use test ads for testing; duplicate accounts are closed. — [AdMob behavioural policies](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2753860?hl=en)
- Incentives: "Google ads may not be placed on apps that promise payment or incentives to users who click on or view ads" (rewarded ad format excepted under its own policy). — [AdMob behavioural policies](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2753860?hl=en)
- Ad refresh: "The refresh rate may not be set to a value outside of the range specified in the SDK." — [AdMob behavioural policies](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2753860?hl=en)
- Disallowed interstitials: "Do not place interstitial ads on app load and when exiting apps"; no interstitial after every user action ("no more than one interstitial ad after every two user actions", also applies to Back button presses); no interstitial immediately after another; no interstitials that prevent viewing core content; "Don't surprise users with interstitial ads" - place only at logical breaks and preload. — [Disallowed interstitial implementations](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201362?hl=en)
- App open ads: "should only appear when the user opens or switches back to an app"; place on top of the splash/loading screen, before the user can interact with content; do not show after content has appeared, do not show another ad immediately before or after, do not show over a screen with banner ads; close option appears after up to 2 seconds (5 with high-engagement ads); apps in Designed for Families cannot use app open ads; discouraged implementations may be treated as invalid activity. — [App open ad guidance](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9341964?hl=en)
- Screens without publisher content: Google-served ads are not allowed on screens without content or with low-value content, under construction, or "used for alerts, navigation or other behavioural purposes"; includes dead-end screens such as thank-you, exit or error pages, and apps where the user is expected to look away (e.g. flashlight). — [Google Publisher Policies: ads on screens without publisher content](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11112688?hl=en)
- Rewarded ads: "must only be served after a user affirmatively and unambiguously opts in"; clear disclosure of required action and reward before each ad; "Direct monetary items may not be offered as rewards under any circumstance"; rewards must be usable only inside the publisher's app and non-transferable; random rewards allowed if odds and possible rewards are disclosed and chance is above 0; rewarded interstitials need an intro screen with a decline option; declining must not impede normal use. — [AdMob rewarded ads policies](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/7313578?hl=en)
- EU consent: publishers must obtain consent from EEA and UK users for cookies/local storage and personalised ads (EU User Consent Policy); AdMob's Privacy & messaging tool supports IAB TCF v2.4 and Google's Additional Consent spec; consent UI must offer "Do not consent", "Consent", "Manage options". — [AdMob: About European regulations messages](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/10114014?hl=en)
- Certified CMP: "a certified CMP integrated with the TCF is required when serving personalized ads to users in the EEA and UK" (from January 16, 2024) and Switzerland (from July 31, 2024); "Only traffic from a certified CMP is eligible for personalized ads"; other traffic may get non-personalised or limited ads. Google offers the UMP SDK to app publishers; under-age users must be tagged with TFUA. — [Google consent management requirements](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/13554116?hl=en)
- app-ads.txt: host at the root of the developer website listed in the Play store listing (`https://<hostname>/app-ads.txt`), IAB Tech Lab format, must contain the AdMob publisher ID line; crawl/verification can take 24 hours or more. — [Set up app-ads.txt for AdMob](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9363762?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Check: "Splash-screen monetisation uses the app open ad format, not an interstitial; shown before content loads, never stacked with other ads."
- Check: "No banner/interstitial on login, loading, error, 'thank you', exit-confirmation or empty-state screens."
- Check: "EEA/UK/Swiss users see a certified-CMP consent form (UMP SDK or other certified CMP) before the first ad request; otherwise expect only limited ads."
- Check: "Developer website in Play listing serves a valid app-ads.txt with the AdMob pub ID."

## 7. Specific monetised categories: loot boxes, gambling, real-money games, NFTs, donations/tipping, gift cards, loyalty, play-to-earn

### Takeaway
Loot boxes need odds disclosure; real-money gambling and daily fantasy sports need a Google-approved, licensed operator, must be free, rated AO, geo-blocked, and must not use Play billing; NFT apps must declare in the Financial features form, cannot sell chances at NFTs of unknown value, and cannot glamorise earnings. Gamified loyalty programs are allowed with fixed, documented ratios and no chance element in games.

### Cited Findings
- Loot boxes: odds must be disclosed in advance of, and in close proximity to, the purchase. — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)
- Gambling apps (online casino, sports betting, horse racing, lotteries, daily fantasy sports) are allowed only if the developer completes Google's application process, is a government operator or licensed operator for each country/state of distribution, stays within licence scope, blocks under-age users and unlicensed locations, is free to download and does not use Play in-app billing, is rated AO (Adult Only) or IARC equivalent, and shows responsible gambling information in app and listing. — [Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)
- Any other app is prohibited from letting users wager, stake or participate with real money (or in-app items bought with money) to win prizes of real-world monetary value; includes UI elements (menus, tabs, buttons, webviews) inviting users to such activity. — [Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)
- DFS apps: only US distribution (or via the gambling-app route elsewhere), accepted through the DFS application, licensed per US state where required, geo-blocked elsewhere, free, no in-app billing, AO-rated. — [Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)
- Online crane game apps may be listed globally since July 12, 2023, subject to law and Google's requirements; other real-money game pilots are described on a separate Help page. — [Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)
- Gamified loyalty programs: benefits must be supplementary to a genuine qualifying purchase, with no fee or wager to participate and no price inflation. Game apps: points only at a fixed, conspicuously documented ratio; no wagering or multiplying by chance or performance; chance-based elements not allowed. Non-game apps: chance/contest rewards allowed if official rules are published in-app, with odds or selection method, number of winners, entry deadline and award date. — [Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)
- Tokenised digital assets (NFTs): apps that sell or let users earn them must declare this in the Financial features declaration (App content page); IAPs representing a tokenised asset must say so; "You may not promote or glamorize any potential earning from playing or trading activities." — [Blockchain-based Content](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13607354?hl=en)
- NFT gamification (apps not approved as gambling apps): must not accept money for a chance to obtain an NFT of unknown value; purchased NFTs must be usable in the game; NFTs may not be wagered or staked for real-world-value prizes. Violations: NFT bundles without disclosed contents/values; pay-to-play social casino rewarding NFTs. — [Blockchain-based Content](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13607354?hl=en)
- Crypto: exchanges and software wallets must be offered through certified services in regulated jurisdictions and comply with local law; on-device crypto mining is prohibited, remote management of mining is permitted. — [Blockchain-based Content](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13607354?hl=en)
- NFTs and billing: GPB is required if tokenised assets are used to buy digital content in-app. — [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Donations: tax-exempt donations must not use GPB (Section 3). Tips: exempt only if 100% goes to creator with no digital perk. Gift cards: exempt from GPB. Loyalty points: earn/redeem free of GPB; selling points requires GPB. — [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en); [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Rewarded-ad payouts: AdMob bars cash rewards and bars ads in apps promising payment for viewing/clicking ads, which constrains "get-paid-to" designs monetised with AdMob. — [AdMob rewarded ads policies](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/7313578?hl=en); [AdMob behavioural policies](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2753860?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Check: "App lets users pay to enter anything with a cash/prize outcome -> must be an approved licensed gambling/DFS app; otherwise remove."
- Check: "Game has loyalty points tied to purchases -> fixed published ratio, no spin-the-wheel on those points."
- Check: "Play-to-earn / NFT app -> Financial features declaration completed; store listing and ads contain no earnings claims; no mystery NFT packs."
- Check: "Donation button for a registered charity -> must use non-Play payment; donation that unlocks in-app perks -> treated as digital purchase, GPB required."

### Gaps
- The country-by-country list of where gambling apps are accepted and which product types are allowed is on the gambling application Help page, which was not retrieved; the policy page itself lists none. I found no confirmation in this session of the current status of India rummy/DFS pilots.
- No dedicated official "play-to-earn" policy was found beyond the Blockchain-based Content policy and the real-money games rules.
- The official donation-eligibility article (what counts as tax-exempt and what documentation Google requires) was not retrieved.

## 8. Merchant / payments profile setup, tax, pricing, supported merchant countries (Pakistan and South Asia)

### Takeaway
Selling paid apps or in-app products requires a Google payments (merchant) profile linked to the Play Console account, tax information and a verified bank account. Google's supported-locations table shows merchant registration available in Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Maldives and Bhutan, but not Nepal or Afghanistan. Pricing templates were discontinued on October 27, 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Supported locations table (as read Nov 2026): Pakistan - developer registration Yes, merchant registration Yes, default currency PKR with footnote that developers in these countries "will be paid in USD or EUR, depending on their location"; India - Yes/Yes, INR; Bangladesh - Yes/Yes, USD; Sri Lanka - Yes/Yes, USD; Maldives - Yes/Yes, USD; Bhutan - Yes/Yes, USD; Nepal - developer Yes, merchant No; Afghanistan - developer Yes, merchant No. Buyers must also be in a supported location to purchase. — [Supported locations for developer and merchant registration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9306917?hl=en)
- A payments profile is required to sell paid apps and in-app purchases and links to Play Console once created; setup starts from the Financial reports / payments settings area. — [Link a Google Play developer account to your payments profile](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/3092739?hl=en)
- Tax information: US merchants provide W-9 data; non-US merchants submit a Certificate of Foreign Status (W-8BEN family); entered under Payments settings. — [Enter merchant tax information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7163598?hl=en)
- Bank account must be added and verified to receive monthly payouts. — [Enter merchant bank account information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7161440?hl=en)
- Sales tax/VAT handling and rates by country are documented separately. — [Tax rates and value-added tax (VAT)](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en)
- Pricing templates: starting October 27, 2025 prices can no longer be managed across products with pricing templates; existing templates were unlinked and prices are set per product. Local prices are generated from the base price; each country has allowed price ranges. — [Set up your app's prices](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6334373?hl=en)
- Developers whose Play earnings exceeded US$1M in the last 12 months can request a maximum price limit of US$10,000 (South Korea stays US$400, Brazil US$8,000). — [Increased price limit](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16247119?hl=en)
- Gambling/DFS apps cannot be paid apps or use in-app billing. — [Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)

### Inferences
- A Pakistan-based developer can register as a merchant and sell paid apps, in-app products and subscriptions worldwide, with payouts in USD (per the table footnote) rather than PKR; a Nepal- or Afghanistan-based developer can publish free apps only (ad monetisation remains possible, subject to AdMob's own country support).
- Because pricing templates are gone, any workflow or documentation that references them is obsolete; price consistency across products must be handled per product or via the Play Developer API.

### Gaps
- The tax and pricing findings in this section came from search-result summaries of the official pages rather than full page reads; details (e.g. exact forms for a Pakistani individual vs company, withholding on US sales) should be confirmed on the live pages.
- Buyer-side status in Pakistan (which payment methods Play accepts from Pakistani users, and whether paid apps are purchasable there) was not verified from an official page.
- Payout thresholds, payout method (wire) and timing for Pakistan were not retrieved.
- AdMob payment-country support for South Asian countries was not researched.

### Inferences
- For a developer whose users are mainly in Pakistan/South Asia, none of the alternative billing or external link programs apply to local users yet; the practical 2026 obligations are PBL 8+ and the standing Payments, Subscriptions and Ads policies. The new fee model will reach those users on September 30, 2027 unless Google changes the timeline.
- Region-gating is now a compliance requirement in its own right: any external-payment link must be shown only to users in a region where the developer is enrolled, which implies using the PBL availability APIs (`isBillingProgramAvailableAsync`, `getBillingChoiceInfoAsync`) rather than IP or locale guesses.
