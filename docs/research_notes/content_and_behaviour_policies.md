# Google Play Developer Program Policies: App Content and Behaviour (as of November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-14. Canonical source used throughout: the single-page **Developer Program Policy, "effective October 29, 2026, unless otherwise stated"** at https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346 (abbreviated **DPP** below). The page is about 294,000 characters and was read in three chunks through a summarising fetch tool, so wording below is near-verbatim paraphrase, not guaranteed character-exact quotation. The answer ID suggested in the brief (16810878) did not resolve to the policy; the current ID is 18265346 (older versioned copies exist at 17517561 and 16933379).

Rule format used below: **IF** the app does X **THEN** it needs Y / is prohibited.

## Key Question 1: What are all the sections of the Developer Program Policy and what does each require?

### Takeaway
The October 29, 2026 policy is organised as Restricted Content (16 sub-areas incl. Financial Services, Gambling, UGC, Health, Blockchain, AI-Generated Content), Intellectual Property, Privacy/Deception/Device Abuse, Monetization and Ads, Store Listing and Promotion, Spam/Functionality/User Experience, Malware, Mobile Unwanted Software, Impersonation, Families, Other Programs, Enforcement, and Play Console Requirements. Most content rules are absolute prohibitions; the checkable "needs Y" obligations cluster in declarations (financial features, health, news, government, target audience, content rating, child-safety self-certification), in-app safety features (report/block, AI flagging, age gating), and listing disclosures.

### Cited Findings

**Section map (order as on the page)**
- Restricted Content contains: Child Endangerment (+ Child Safety Standards Policy), Inappropriate Content (Sexual Content and Profanity, Hate Speech, Violence, Violent Extremism, Sensitive Events, Bullying and Harassment, Dangerous Products, Marijuana, Tobacco and Alcohol), Age-Restricted Content and Functionality, Financial Services, Real-Money Gambling/Games/Contests, Illegal Activities, User Generated Content, Health Content and Services, Blockchain-based Content, AI-Generated Content. Then Intellectual Property; Privacy, Deception and Device Abuse (User Data, Permissions, Device and Network Abuse, Deceptive Behavior, Misrepresentation, Target API Level, Use of SDKs); Malware; Impersonation; Mobile Unwanted Software; Monetization and Ads (Payments, Ads, Subscriptions, Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Program); Store Listing and Promotion (App Promotion, Metadata, User Ratings/Reviews/Installs, Content Ratings, News & Magazines); Spam, Functionality, and User Experience; Other Programs (Android Instant Apps, Android Emoji Policy); Families; Enforcement; Play Console Requirements; Account Transfers — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- The policy page header reads "(effective October 29, 2026, unless otherwise stated)" — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Since July 15, 2026 some policies use a standardized numbering system (e.g. Deceptive Behavior 2.1, 2.2.1, 3.1), with more policies to be renumbered over time — [Policy announcement July 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731)

**Child Endangerment**
- IF the app does not prohibit users from creating, uploading or distributing content that facilitates child exploitation/abuse (all CSAM) THEN immediate removal; Google may report to NCMEC. Prohibited uses: inappropriate interaction targeted at a child, grooming, sexualization of a minor, sextortion, child trafficking — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- IF the app appeals to children THEN it may not contain adult themes (excessive violence, blood, gore; depicting or encouraging harmful/dangerous activities); apps promoting negative body or self-image (plastic surgery, weight loss, cosmetic adjustment for entertainment) are not allowed — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- **Child Safety Standards Policy** — IF the app is an Anonymous Chat, Random Chat, Social or Dating app THEN it must self-certify in Play Console and: (1) publish standards explicitly prohibiting CSAE in publicly accessible documents (ToS/community guidelines); (2) provide an in-app mechanism for user feedback/concerns/reports; (3) take appropriate action on actual knowledge of CSAM incl. removal; (4) comply with child-safety laws incl. a process to report confirmed CSAM to NCMEC or the regional authority; (5) designate a child-safety point of contact able to speak to enforcement/review procedures — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346); help page linked from policy: [Child Safety Standards](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14747720)
- Extension of the Child Safety Standards policy to anonymous chat and random chat apps was announced July 15, 2026 with deadline August 26, 2026 — [Policy Deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780)

**Inappropriate Content**
- Sexual Content and Profanity: prohibited — sexual content or profanity incl. pornography, content/services intended to be sexually gratifying, apps appearing to promote sexual acts for compensation, sexually predatory content, non-consensual sexual content. Exception: nudity whose primary purpose is educational, documentary, scientific or artistic (EDSA) and not gratuitous. Listed violations: sexual nudity or suggestive poses with subject nude/blurred/minimally clothed; depictions of sex acts or sexualized body parts; sexual aids, sex guides, illegal sexual themes, fetishes; lewd/profane content incl. slurs or sexual keywords in listing or app; bestiality; escort services or "sugar dating"; apps claiming to undress people or see through clothing "even as a prank"; creepshots, hidden camera, deepfake non-consensual sexual content — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Catalog apps (books/video) may carry sexual-content titles only IF all hold: minor fraction of catalog; not actively promoted; no child endangerment/pornography/illegal sexual content; minors' access restricted — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Hate Speech: no promoting violence or inciting hatred based on race or ethnic origin, religion, disability, age, nationality, veteran status, sexual orientation, gender, gender identity, caste, immigration status or other characteristic associated with systemic discrimination. Violations: asserting a protected group is inhuman/inferior; hateful slurs, stereotypes or theories; encouraging discrimination; hate symbols, flags, insignias — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Violence: no gratuitous violence or dangerous activities; fictional violence in a game context (cartoons, hunting, fishing) generally allowed. Violations: graphic realistic violence or violent threats against a person or animal; promoting self-harm, suicide, eating disorders, choking games — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Violent Extremism: terrorist/dangerous organisations may not publish apps for any purpose; no content planning, preparing or glorifying violence against civilians; educational/documentary content must carry EDSA context — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Sensitive Events: no capitalising on or insensitivity toward civil emergencies, natural disasters, public health emergencies, conflicts, deaths. Violations: insensitivity about a real person's death; denying a well-documented major tragic event; appearing to profit with no discernible benefit to victims — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Bullying and Harassment: no apps containing or facilitating threats, harassment or bullying (bullying conflict victims, extortion/blackmail, public humiliation, harassing victims of tragic events) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Dangerous Products: no facilitating sale of explosives, firearms, ammunition or certain accessories; no manufacturing instructions incl. converting to automatic fire. Restricted accessories include bump stocks, gatling triggers, drop-in auto sears, conversion kits, and magazines or belts carrying **more than 30 rounds** — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Marijuana: no facilitating sale of marijuana or marijuana products "regardless of legality" (in-app cart ordering, arranging delivery/pickup, THC products incl. CBD oils containing THC) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Tobacco and Alcohol: no facilitating sale of tobacco/nicotine products (e-cigarettes, vape pens, nicotine pouches); no encouraging illegal or inappropriate use of alcohol/tobacco. Violations: depicting or encouraging use/sale to minors; implying tobacco improves social, sexual, professional, intellectual or athletic standing; favourably portraying binge or competitive drinking; ads/promotions/prominent featuring of tobacco. Exceptions: limited tobacco sales in food/grocery delivery apps in certain regions with age-gating (e.g. ID check at delivery); nicotine-cessation aids with age-gating — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Age-Restricted Content and Functionality** (new policy announced October 30, 2025; expanded July 15, 2026)
- IF the app (1) facilitates real-money gambling, games or contests, or (2) has core functionality of matchmaking/dating, or (3) has core functionality of randomly connecting users to communicate spontaneously with strangers, or (4) has core functionality of anonymous communication where one or more identities are deliberately hidden THEN it must use the Play Console tools to block minors — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- IF dating/matchmaking is only incidental THEN Restrict Minor Access is not mandatory but the developer must implement its own robust age gating for those features; "Simple age gates that can be easily circumvented may not be considered reasonable" — [Requirements for apps with incidental dating or matchmaking features](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16838200)
- How Restrict Minor Access works: select "18 and over" as the only target age group and tick the box; "users determined to be under 18 will not be able to search for, download or purchase the app"; existing minor users keep the app but cannot renew subscriptions or make new purchases — [Manage target audience and app content settings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en)

**Illegal Activities**
- No apps facilitating or promoting illegal activities. Violations: facilitating sale/purchase of illegal drugs; depicting or encouraging drug, alcohol or tobacco use/sale by minors; instructions for growing or manufacturing illegal drugs — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Intellectual Property**
- No infringing trademark, copyright, patent, trade secret or other proprietary rights, nor encouraging/inducing infringement. Developers with written permission to use third-party IP should contact the Google Play team before submission; developers "may be asked to prove" rights — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Copyright violation examples: cover art for albums/video games/books; marketing images from movies/TV/games; artwork from comics, cartoons, movies, music videos, TV; college or professional sports team logos; photos from a public figure's social media; professional images of public figures; fan art indistinguishable from the original; soundboards of copyrighted audio; full reproductions or translations of non-public-domain books — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Encouraging infringement examples: streaming apps that let users download local copies without authorisation; apps (including via listing text or screenshots) encouraging streaming/downloading copyrighted music/video — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Trademark: no use likely to cause confusion about source. Counterfeit: no selling/promoting goods bearing a mark identical or substantially indistinguishable from another's — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Impersonation**
- No impersonating another developer, company, entity or app; no implying a non-existent relationship via icon, description, title or in-app elements. Violations: developer names falsely suggesting a relationship with Google; national emblems implying government affiliation; copying a business's logo to look official; titles/icons confusingly similar to existing products (e.g. a cryptocurrency site's logo, a TV show's characters and title); "Official" claims without rights; violating Android Brand Guidelines — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- A Help Center FAQ link was added to the Impersonation policy on July 10, 2025 — [Policy announcement July 10, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16296680)

**Malware**
- Malware = any code that could put a user, their data or device at risk; applies to third-party code/SDKs. Categories: Backdoors, Billing Fraud (SMS, call, toll fraud), Stalkerware, Denial of Service, Hostile Downloaders, Non-Android Threat, Phishing, Elevated Privilege Abuse, Ransomware, Rooting, Spam, Spyware, Trojan, Riskware (evasion via obfuscation, dynamic code loading, cloaking to serve different/fake functionality) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- "Maskware" was renamed "Riskware" on October 30, 2025 — [Policy announcement October 30, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16550159)
- IF the app is a monitoring app (parental/enterprise) THEN it must use the IsMonitoringTool flag, show a persistent notification, disclose monitoring in the store description, must not present itself as a spying tool or hide tracking, and must not be used to track e.g. a spouse — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Mobile Unwanted Software (MUwS)**
- Principles: transparent behaviour and clear disclosures (deliver all promised functionality; explain and get approval for significant system changes; do not misrepresent device state such as falsely claiming infection; no invalid ad traffic; no impersonation); protect user data (do not request or trick users into disabling Play Protect, nor reward doing so); do not harm the mobile experience (no ads outside the app without easy dismissal/consent/attribution; clear uninstall; do not mimic OS/app prompts or suppress alerts) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Hostile downloader threshold: flagged if at least **5%** of downloaded apps are MUwS with a minimum of **500** observed downloads (25 observed MUwS downloads); major browsers and file-sharing apps exempt when downloads are user-initiated — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- System notifications may only be used for integral app features; an ad delivered through a system notification is a violation. Social engineering (pretending to be another app) is prohibited — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Device and Network Abuse**
- No interfering with, disrupting, damaging or accessing without authorisation devices, servers, networks, APIs or services. Apps may not self-update outside Play's mechanism or download executable code (dex, JAR, .so) from outside Play; runtime-loaded interpreted code (JavaScript, Python, Lua) must not enable policy violations — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Listed violations: blocking/interfering with another app's ads; game cheats affecting other apps; hacking/circumvention guides; using a service or API against its terms; bypassing power management without eligibility; third-party proxy services unless that is the primary user-facing purpose; installing other apps without prior consent; linking to malware; webviews with a JavaScript interface loading untrusted content; full-screen intents forcing interaction with disruptive ads; breaking sandbox protections — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Store-listing items that are content rules**
- Metadata: title **30 characters or less**; no emojis, emoticons or repeated special characters in title/icon/developer name; no ALL CAPS unless brand; no performance/ranking claims ("App of the year", "#1", "Popular"), price/promo claims ("10% off", "free for limited time only") or Play programme claims ("Editor's choice", "New"); no unattributed or anonymous testimonials; no misleading icon symbols (new-message dot, download symbol) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Content Ratings: apps without an IARC content rating are not allowed; questionnaire required for every new and every existing active app; redo it after material content changes; ads shown must not be significantly more mature than the app's primary content — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- July 15, 2026 clarification: "Unrated apps are not allowed on Google Play" — [Policy announcement July 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731)
- Ratings/reviews/installs: no incentivised or fraudulent ratings; no incentivising installs of other apps as the app's main function — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Subscriptions (content-transparency parts only)**
- Offer screens must clearly disclose terms, cost, billing frequency, auto-renewal, and whether a subscription is required to use the app, without extra user action; subscriptions must give sustained or recurring value; one-time benefits may not be disguised as subscriptions. Violations include annual plans that most prominently show the monthly cost, incompletely localised pricing, SKU named "Free Trial" for an auto-renewing charge, reducing benefits (1,000 gems first month, then 1 gem), no dismiss button — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Apps selling subscriptions must disclose how to manage/cancel and provide an easy online cancellation method in account settings — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- New October 29, 2026: subscriptions with ongoing eligibility-based discounts (e.g. student plans) "must clearly disclose eligibility criteria, the verification process" and post-eligibility pricing/benefit changes; deadline January 27, 2027 — [Policy Deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780)

**Play Console account-type requirement tied to content category**
- An **Organization** developer account is required for: financial products and services (banking, loans, stock trading, investment funds, crypto software wallets and exchanges); health apps (medical apps, human-subjects research apps); apps approved to use the VpnService class; government apps (developed by or for a government agency) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Before submitting: provide accurate metadata, a privacy policy and Data safety section, an active demo account/login details for review, and register package names for Android developer verification — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Enforcement reach**
- Policies apply to any content an app displays or links to, including ads and hosted or linked UGC, and to the developer name and listed website. Enforcement actions: Rejection, Removal, Suspension (counts as strike), Limited Visibility, Limited Regions, Restricted Developer Account, Account Termination (related accounts too) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- A 180-day appeal window applies to account terminations (announced October 30, 2025) — [Policy announcement October 30, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16550159)

### Inferences
- For a pre-submission checker, the DPP sections split into (a) pure content prohibitions needing human/AI content review, and (b) declaration/feature obligations that can be tested mechanically (declaration present? report button present? disclaimer string present in description? title length ≤ 30?).
- Because policies apply to linked content and SDK behaviour, a checker must also inspect outbound links, ad SDKs and embedded webviews, not just first-party screens.

### Gaps
- Individual per-section Help Center URLs were confirmed only for some sections (Spam 9899034, Functionality 9898783, News 9935326, Families 9893335, Child Safety Standards 14747720). For the remaining sections the unified DPP page is cited; per-section answer IDs were not individually verified in this session.
- Exact character-level quotations could not be guaranteed because the fetch tool paraphrases; anything in quotation marks above was returned as a quote by the tool but should be re-verified before being presented as verbatim Google text.
- No dedicated "Social/dating apps" section exists in the DPP; rules for them are spread across Child Safety Standards, Age-Restricted Content, UGC and Families (see above).

## Key Question 2: What exactly do the Spam, Minimum Functionality, Broken Functionality, Webview and Affiliate Spam, and Repetitive Content policies say?

### Takeaway
These rules are short, threshold-free and judged qualitatively: an app must offer app-specific functionality and original value, must install/load/respond, must not be a bare webview of a site the developer lacks permission for or an affiliate funnel, and must not duplicate other apps (including the developer's own near-identical apps). Google publishes no numeric thresholds (no minimum screen count, content volume or crash rate) in the policy text.

### Cited Findings
- **Spam (umbrella)**: apps may not spam users or Google Play, including unsolicited messages or repetitive or low-quality apps — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346); section page: [Spam](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034?hl=en)
- **Message Spam**: IF the app sends SMS, email or other messages on the user's behalf THEN the user must be able to confirm content and recipients. Violation example: pressing "Share" sends messages without a confirmation step — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- **Webviews and Affiliate Spam**: "We don't allow apps whose primary purpose is to drive affiliate traffic to a website or provide a webview of a website without permission from the website owner or administrator." Violation examples: an app whose main purpose is referral traffic to earn sign-up/purchase credit; an app that is primarily a webview without permission, e.g. "Ted's Shopping Deals" which "simply provides a webview of Google Shopping" — [Spam](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034?hl=en)
- **Repetitive Content**: no apps that merely replicate experiences of apps already on Google Play; apps should add unique content or services. Violations: copying content from other apps without adding original value; creating multiple highly similar apps — if each has small content volume, developers "should consider creating a single app that aggregates all the content" — [Spam](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034?hl=en)
- **Made for Ads** (sits in the Ads policy but is a common spam-type rejection): no apps that show interstitials repeatedly to distract from the app, e.g. an interstitial after "Start app" and another after the next click — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- **Functionality, Content, and User Experience (umbrella)**: apps must be stable, responsive and engaging; apps that crash, lack basic utility, lack engaging content or behave inconsistently are not allowed — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346); section page: [Functionality, Content, and User Experience](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898783?hl=en)
- **1. Limited Functionality and Content** (the rule usually called "Minimum Functionality"): "We do not allow apps that only have limited functionality and content." Violation examples: static apps without app-specific functionality (text-only or PDF-file apps); apps with very little content and no engaging experience (e.g. single-wallpaper apps); apps designed to do nothing or with no function — [Functionality, Content, and User Experience](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898783?hl=en)
- **2. Broken Functionality**: no apps that crash, force close, freeze or otherwise function abnormally. Violation examples: apps that don't install; install but don't load; load but are unresponsive — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Reviewers need working access: developers must provide an active demo account, login details and any other resources needed for review — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Play's pre-review checks note that an issue making the app non-functional may lead reviewers to reject it under app-quality policies such as Minimum Functionality — [Detect app issues early with pre-review checks](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14807773)
- Families cross-reference: child-directed apps likewise must not merely provide a webview or have a primary purpose of driving affiliate traffic without permission (Families Policy Requirement 2) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- White-label/template developers have a dedicated best-practices article (published February 10, 2025) — [Best Practices for White Label Developers](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15884185?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Concrete pre-submission checks derivable from the text: (1) Is the app only a WebView of a website? If yes, does the developer own the site or hold written permission from its owner, and does the app add native functionality? (2) Is the primary flow an affiliate/referral link? (3) Is the app a single static document/PDF/one wallpaper? (4) Does the developer already publish near-identical apps (same template, different content)? If so merge them. (5) Does a clean install launch, load and respond, and do review credentials work?
- A site-wrapper app for the developer's own business site satisfies the "permission" limb of the webview rule but can still fail Limited Functionality if it offers nothing app-specific; the policy text treats these as two separate tests.
- Because there are no numeric thresholds, borderline apps are decided at reviewer discretion; adding native features (offline content, notifications tied to a core feature, account functions) is the practical mitigation, though Google does not enumerate what suffices.

### Gaps
- Google does not publish quantitative thresholds for "limited functionality", "very little content", crash rates for Broken Functionality, or the degree of similarity that makes apps "highly similar". None were found on official pages.
- A search snippet stated that Google "clarified our Spam policies to explicitly prohibit repetitive content"; the date of that clarification could not be confirmed from the fetched pages (it appears to pre-date 2025).
- How Google verifies "permission from the website owner" (document type, submission channel) is not specified in the policy text; a likely channel is the advance notice form, but this was not confirmed for webview cases.

## Key Question 3: What do the Deceptive Behavior, Misleading Claims, Device Settings Changes, Manipulated Media and Misrepresentation policies require?

### Takeaway
Everything the listing and app claim must be true, complete and consistent with actual behaviour; functionality must not be hidden, dormant or altered for review; device settings changes need consent and easy reversal; synthetic/altered media needs disclaimers or watermarks; and the developer must not conceal identity, origin or affiliation (especially government). The October 29, 2026 update added an explicit statement that developers and apps may not manipulate the app review process.

### Cited Findings
- **General Deceptive Behavior**: no deceiving users or enabling dishonest behaviour, including apps that are "functionally impossible"; all metadata must accurately disclose functionality; do not mimic OS or other-app functionality or warnings; settings changes need the user's knowledge and consent and must be reversible; manipulating Play review, including misleading reviewers about purpose or functionality, is strictly prohibited — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- October 29, 2026 clarification: Deceptive Behavior "clarifies that developers and apps may not manipulate the app review process" — [Policy announcement October 29, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18100766)
- **1. Misleading Claims**: no false or misleading information in description, title, icon or screenshots. Violations: misrepresenting functionality (racing game that is a puzzle; "antivirus" that is a text guide); claiming impossible functions even as a prank (phone breathalyzer); improper categorisation or rating; demonstrably deceptive content that may interfere with voting/elections; falsely claiming government affiliation or providing government services without authorisation; falsely claiming to be an established entity's official app — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- **2. Deceptive Device Settings Changes**: 2.1 no changes to device settings or features outside the app without knowledge and consent (system and browser settings, bookmarks, shortcuts, icons, widgets, home screen). 2.2 not allowed: 2.2.1 consented changes that are not easily reversible; 2.2.2 changes made as a service to third parties or for advertising; 2.2.3 misleading users into removing/disabling third-party apps or changing settings; 2.2.4 encouraging or incentivising such removal or changes unless part of a verifiable security service — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- **3. Enabling Dishonest Behavior**: 3.1 no apps that help users mislead others or are functionally deceptive, incl. generating or facilitating IDs, social security numbers, passports, diplomas, credit cards, bank accounts, driver's licences. 3.2 extra resources (e.g. game assets) may be downloaded only if necessary, and the app must prompt the user and disclose download size first. 3.3 calling the app a "prank" or "for entertainment purposes" gives no exemption. Violations: mimicking other apps/sites to harvest credentials; showing unverified or real phone numbers/contacts/addresses of non-consenting people; core functionality that varies by geography or device without prominent listing disclosure; significant changes between versions without alerting users or updating the listing; modifying/obfuscating behaviour during review; CDN downloads without prompt and size disclosure — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- **4. Manipulated Media**: no promoting or creating false or misleading claims through imagery, audio, video or text, especially where harmful regarding a sensitive event, politics, social issues or public concern. Exceptions: public-interest content, clearly artificial imagery, manipulated media with user-facing disclaimers or watermarks, obvious satire/parody. Violations: using public figures or sensitive-event media to advertise media-altering features in the listing; altering clips to mimic news broadcasts with real outlet names/logos and no disclaimer/watermark; apps whose sole purpose is creating misleading media — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Manipulated Media exceptions and violation examples were clarified April 10, 2025 (no change to enforcement standards) — [Policy announcement April 10, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15899442)
- **5. Behavior Transparency**: functionality must be reasonably clear to users; no hidden, dormant or undocumented features; "Techniques to evade app reviews are not allowed"; Google may require extra details for user safety, system integrity or policy compliance — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- **Misrepresentation**: no apps or developer accounts that impersonate any person or organisation or that misrepresent or conceal ownership or primary purpose; no coordinated activity to mislead users, including misrepresenting or concealing country of origin while directing content at users in another country; no coordinating with other apps, sites, developers or accounts to conceal identity where content relates to politics, social issues or matters of public concern — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Misrepresenting any app information in Play Console (e.g. target audience) may result in removal or suspension — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- **Government information (applied Misleading Claims)**: IF the app communicates government information and is NOT government-affiliated THEN it must (a) show easy-to-see information sources in the app description / store listing page and (b) make clear that it does not represent a government or political entity. Sources should be verifiable, e.g. a .gov URL for US government information or a .go.jp URL for Japanese government information — [Requirements for apps that communicate government information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9514050?hl=en)
- IF the app is authorised to facilitate a government process (voting, ID validation) THEN Google may require proof before approval, and the proof must come from the relevant government entity with official contact information — [Requirements for apps that communicate government information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9514050?hl=en)
- Since January 31, 2023 developers must complete the **Government apps declaration** in Play Console (Policy and programs > App content); completing it makes the app eligible for an official endorsement display if further criteria are verified — [Requirements for apps that communicate government information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9514050?hl=en)
- App Promotion (related): the app may not benefit from deceptive promotion such as ads resembling system alerts, redirects to Play without informed action, or unsolicited SMS promotion; developers are responsible for ad networks and affiliates — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

### Inferences
- Checks: every claim in title/short description/screenshots must be demonstrable in the build submitted; any feature gated by region, device, remote config or account type must be disclosed in the listing and reachable by the reviewer (otherwise it risks being read as hidden functionality or review evasion).
- Remote-config-driven UI swaps after approval are a direct risk under Behavior Transparency, Enabling Dishonest Behavior ("change significantly between versions") and the new review-manipulation clarification.
- An app that surfaces tax, visa, ID, benefits or similar public-service information from a non-government developer needs both a source link and a non-affiliation disclaimer in the store description; apps using national emblems/flags in the icon additionally risk Impersonation.

### Gaps
- The exact new wording inserted into Deceptive Behavior on October 29, 2026 was not retrieved verbatim; only the announcement's summary is cited.
- The government-information help page does not list explicit non-compliant examples or state whether the disclaimer must also appear in-app (it specifies description and store listing).

## Key Question 4: What are the Families policy and "Target audience and content" requirements?

### Takeaway
Every app must declare target age groups; including any under-13 group (or being judged child-appealing by Google) triggers eight numbered Families Policy Requirements plus strict ad rules (Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs only, no interest-based ads, 5-second closable rule, no launch interstitials). Mixed-audience apps need a neutral age screen; since August 26, 2026 anonymous-chat apps may not target children at all.

### Cited Findings

**Target audience and content declaration**
- Target age options: Ages 5 and under, 6-8, 9-12, 13-15, 16-17, 18 and over; multiple selections allowed only if the app is designed for and appropriate for each selected group — [Manage target audience and app content settings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en)
- Outcomes: "designed primarily for children under 13" → must comply with Families Policy Requirements and use only Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs; "designed for everyone, including children" → Families requirements apply and ads to children/unknown-age users must come from self-certified SDKs; "not designed for children" → general policies and DDA only; 18+ only → may (or under Age-Restricted policy must) enable Restrict Minor Access — [Manage target audience and app content settings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en)
- Apps for babies, toddlers and preschoolers should select only "Ages 5 & Under"; choose adult+child groups only if truly designed for all ages; imagery or terminology that could be seen as targeting children may affect Google's assessment, and Google may review the declaration independently — [Google Play Families Policies](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)
- IF store-listing marketing (youthful animation, young characters) suggests the app targets children while declared otherwise THEN Google Play may reject it; the developer must remove those elements or change the audience to under-13 and comply with Families Policy. Target-audience review may take up to 7 days or more in exceptional cases — [Manage target audience and app content settings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en)
- The developer must declare whether the app contains ads before completing the section; if ads are served to children, the Ads section asks about the Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Program or a neutral age screen — [Manage target audience and app content settings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en)
- "Children" is not given one global age: "The word 'children' can mean different things in different locales and in different contexts"; consult legal counsel — [Google Play Families Policies](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)

**Neutral age screen**
- Definition: verifies age without encouraging users to falsify it; correct example — free entry of month, day and year of birth; incorrect — presetting the birth date to the required age or indicating that a certain age is required to access parts of the app — [Manage target audience and app content settings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en)
- Mixed-audience apps "must include a neutral age screen to ensure children only see non-personalized ads" — [Google Play Families Policies](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)

**Families Policy Requirements (apply if children are one target audience)**
- 1 App content: content accessible to children must be appropriate for children — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- 2 App functionality: must not merely provide a webview or have a primary purpose of driving affiliate traffic without permission — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- 3 Play Console answers (Target Audience and Content, Data safety, IARC questionnaire) must be accurate and kept updated — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- 4 Data practices: disclose collection of children's personal/sensitive information incl. via APIs/SDKs; child-only apps must not transmit AAID, SIM serial, build serial, BSSID, MAC, SSID, IMEI or IMSI, should not request AD_ID when targeting API 33+, may not request location permission or collect precise location, must not request the phone number from TelephonyManager, and must use Companion Device Manager for Bluetooth; mixed-audience apps must not transmit those identifiers from children or users of unknown age — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- 5 APIs and SDKs: child-only apps may not contain APIs/SDKs not approved for child-directed services; mixed-audience apps may use them only behind a neutral age screen or with no child data collected, and must not require children to access content through them — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- 6 Augmented Reality: show a safety warning on AR launch (parental supervision, awareness of physical hazards) and do not require devices advised against for children — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- 7 Social apps and features: in-app online-safety reminder before children exchange freeform media/information; adult action required before children exchange personal information; adult management controls for social features. "Adult action" = verification such as PIN, password, birthdate, email verification, photo ID, credit card or SSN. Social apps whose main focus is chatting with strangers or anonymously (chat roulette, dating, kids-focused open chat rooms, anonymous or "blindfold" apps) must not target children — [Google Play Families Policies](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)
- 8 Legal compliance: app and its APIs/SDKs must comply with COPPA, GDPR and other applicable law — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Listed violations: listing promotes play for children but content is adult-only; APIs whose terms prohibit child-directed use; glamorising alcohol, tobacco or controlled substances; real or simulated gambling; violence, gore or shocking content; dating services or sexual/marital advice; links to sites violating Play policies; mature ads shown to children — [Google Play Families Policies](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)
- July 15, 2026 update: Families Policy Requirements expanded to "prohibit developers of anonymous chat apps from targeting children"; deadline August 26, 2026 — [Policy Deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780)

**Families ads and monetization**
- For child or unknown-age users: only Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs; no interest-based advertising or remarketing; child-appropriate ad content; compliant formats — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Prohibited formats: disruptive full-screen ads without a clear dismiss (ad walls); ads interfering with use that are not closable after **5 seconds** (including rewarded/opt-in); interstitials immediately on app launch; multiple ad placements on a page (more than one banner or video ad, banners with multiple offers); ads not clearly distinguishable from content (offerwalls, immersive ads); shocking or emotionally manipulative tactics; dismiss buttons that trigger another ad; failing to distinguish virtual coins from real money in IAP — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Inappropriate ad content for children: unsuitable media, video-game/software ads, controlled or harmful substances, gambling incl. simulated gambling and sweepstakes, adult/sexually suggestive, dating, violent/graphic — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- For general (non-Families) apps the comparable limit is **15 seconds**: unexpected full-screen interstitials, and full-screen interstitials not closable after 15 seconds, are prohibited (opt-in/rewarded exempt) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Program / Policy**
- Child-only apps must use only self-certified SDK versions; mixed-audience apps must age-screen and ensure child-directed ads come only from self-certified SDKs; in-house cross-promotion and direct-sold inventory are exempt from self-certification but must still comply on content and data — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- SDK obligations: register on Play SDK Console; prohibit objectionable ad content; rate creatives at least Everyone/Mature; support per-request or per-app child-directed treatment disabling personalised ads, IBA and remarketing; review creatives for real-time bidding and propagate privacy indicators; supply a test app, English ToS, privacy policy and integration guide; self-certify each new release; mediation platforms must use self-certified SDKs — [Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12918983?hl=en)
- October 29, 2026: the program is open to new applicants; participating SDKs and self-certified versions are listed in the Help Center and SDK Index — [Policy announcement October 29, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18100766)

**Teacher Approved**
- Apps complying with Families policies may opt in to be rated for the Teacher Approved program; inclusion is not guaranteed. Eligible: apps primarily for children and apps designed for several age groups including children — [Manage target audience and app content settings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Checks: declared age groups vs. art style/characters in the listing; if any under-13 group is selected then scan the build for non-certified ad SDKs, AD_ID permission, location permission, launch interstitials, more than one ad per screen, and non-closable ads over 5 seconds.
- The current DPP no longer describes a separately named "Designed for Families" opt-in programme as the gate; the trigger is the Target audience declaration, with Teacher Approved as the optional quality programme. The gambling-ads rule still references apps "enrolled in the Designed for Families program", so the legacy name persists in at least one place.
- An adult-oriented app with cartoon-style branding should explicitly select only 13+/18+ groups and avoid child-directed imagery in its listing to avoid forced Families classification.

### Gaps
- The Teacher Approved rating criteria (age-appropriateness, quality, enrichment, delight) and review timeline were not retrieved from an official page in this session.
- The "appeals to children" question and the "Not designed for children" store label, which earlier versions of the target-audience flow included, were not found on the current help page as fetched; whether they still exist in the Console UI is unconfirmed.
- The Families page as fetched was truncated, so any 2026 wording on anonymous-chat apps beyond Requirement 7 could not be read in full.
- Some locale copies of the help pages use "Expert Approved" rather than "Teacher Approved"; which name is current in each locale was not resolved.

## Key Question 5: What category-specific requirements exist (health, financial services by country, VPN, news, government, gambling, crypto)?

### Takeaway
Regulated categories combine a Play Console declaration, an Organization account, listing/in-app disclosures and country-specific licence proof. Personal-loan rules are the most detailed (no loans due in ≤60 days, eight banned permissions, US APR cap of 36%, licence documents for India, Indonesia, Philippines, Nigeria, Kenya, Pakistan, Thailand), and every app on an account must complete the Financial features declaration even if it has none.

### Cited Findings

**Financial Services — general**
- Apps must not expose users to deceptive or harmful financial products; must comply with state and local regulations in each targeted region; any app with financial features must complete the Financial features declaration in Play Console — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Since October 30, 2025 every app on the account must complete the Financial features declaration, "even apps without financial features"; apps cannot be updated until it is completed — [Policy announcement October 30, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16550159)
- Binary options trading apps are not allowed — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Personal loans**
- Definition: lending to an individual consumer on a nonrecurring basis, not for a fixed asset or education; includes personal, payday, peer-to-peer and title loans; excludes mortgages, car loans, revolving credit (credit cards, personal lines of credit). Applies to direct lenders, lead generators and connectors — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- IF personal-loan app THEN: category "Finance"; disclose in app metadata the minimum and maximum repayment period, maximum APR (interest plus fees, annualised), a representative example of total loan cost, and a privacy policy comprehensively disclosing access/collection/use/sharing of personal and sensitive data — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Prohibited: personal loans requiring full repayment in **60 days or less** ("short-term personal loans") — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Prohibited permissions for personal-loan, loan-facilitation, accessory-loan (calculators, guides) and EWA apps: READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, READ_CONTACTS, ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION, READ_PHONE_NUMBERS, READ_MEDIA_VIDEO, QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES, WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Developer accounts must be linked to licences/documentation proving ability to provide financial products; Google may request more — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Personal Loans policy was extended to line-of-credit apps on April 10, 2025 — [Policy announcement April 10, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15899442)

**Earned Wage Access (EWA)**
- Features: repayment via payroll deduction/autopay with no extra interest, penalties or fees on failure; limited to wages already earned in the current pay period; no interest, fee "likely in the range of" $1–$5 per transaction or 1–5% of the advance; typically no credit-bureau reporting. Required: category "Finance"; disclose repayment terms, all fees, a representative total-cost example, comprehensive privacy policy — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- July 15, 2026 clarification reframed EWA requirements so EWA apps meet the same transparency and privacy standards as other financial apps — [Policy announcement July 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731)

**Country-specific personal-loan requirements** (documents submitted via the Financial features declaration)
- United States: personal loans with APR of **36% or higher** not allowed; maximum APR displayed and calculated consistently with TILA — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- India: only apps on the RBI list "Digital lending apps (DLAs) deployed by Regulated Entities" may be submitted, with licence submitted; a platform for registered NBFCs/banks must declare this and prominently disclose all NBFC and bank names in the app description — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346); India list guidance linked from policy: [answer/16604194](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16604194)
- India change announced October 30, 2025: apps must be on the government-published list of approved digital lending apps; all new personal-loan apps must comply as of October 30, 2025 — [Policy announcement October 30, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16550159)
- Indonesia: services under OJK Regulation No. 77/POJK.01/2016 (as amended) must submit a copy of a valid licence — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Philippines: Online Lending Platforms need an SEC Registration Number and Certificate of Authority (CA) Number from the Philippines SEC; app description must disclose Corporate Name, Business Name, PSEC Registration Number and CA; lending-based crowdfunding must transact through PSEC-registered CF Intermediaries — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Nigeria: Digital Money Lenders must comply with the FCCPC's Limited Interim Regulatory/Registration Framework and Guidelines for Digital Lending, 2022 and hold a verifiable FCCPC approval letter; loan aggregators must provide documentation plus contact details for every partnered DML — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Kenya: Digital Credit Providers must be registered and licensed by the Central Bank of Kenya and provide the licence; facilitating platforms must provide each partner's DCP licence; only entities in the CBK Directory of Digital Credit Providers are accepted — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Pakistan: each NBFC lender may publish **only one** Digital Lending App (more risks termination of the developer account and associated accounts); proof of SECP approval required to offer or facilitate digital lending; short-term loans not permitted except rare cases explicitly allowed by Pakistani law — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- July 10, 2025: clarified that only Pakistan has limited exceptions allowing short-term loans (under 60 days), in line with local law — [Policy announcement July 10, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16296680)
- Thailand: apps with interest rates at or above **15%** need a Bank of Thailand licence (personal loan provider or nano finance) and/or a Ministry of Finance Pico/Pico-plus licence; all Thailand personal-loan listings must display licensed provider name(s), developer legal entity name, maximum interest rates and all fees; apps below 15% must display "This is a non-regulated loan provider under the BoT/FPO." — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Thailand listing requirements took effect immediately for new apps on January 28, 2026; existing apps had until March 4, 2026 — [Policy announcement January 28, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16797260)

**Real-money gambling, games and contests**
- Gambling apps allowed only in select countries for approved governmental or licensed operators that completed Google's application; requirements: valid licence per country/state and per product type; prevent under-age use; geo-block unlicensed areas; free to download, not a paid app, no Google Play in-app billing; rated AO or IARC equivalent; responsible-gambling information in app and listing — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346); application form: [gambling contact form](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/contact/gambling)
- All other apps: no content or service letting users wager, stake or participate with real money (including in-app items bought with money) to win prizes of real-world monetary value. Violations: games accepting money for a chance at a physical/monetary prize; navigation elements or CTAs like "BET!", "REGISTER!", "COMPETE!" for cash prizes; apps managing wagers, in-app currencies, winnings or deposits — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Gamified loyalty programs: benefits must be subordinate to a genuine separate qualifying purchase, not purchasable, no entry fee/wager, no inflated prices. Game apps: fixed-ratio points only, ratio shown conspicuously, no chance-based or performance-multiplied rewards. Non-game apps: chance-based rewards allowed with in-app official rules, disclosed odds or selection method, fixed number of winners, entry deadline and award date — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Gambling ads inside non-gambling apps allowed only if: lawful and licensed locally; not shown to known under-18s; app not in Designed for Families and not targeting under-18s; responsible-gambling info on landing page; app is not a simulated-gambling app, not a gambling companion (odds/score tracking for betting), and does not itself direct users to real-money gambling — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Daily Fantasy Sports: US-only (or via gambling-app eligibility elsewhere), application required, state licences, geo-blocking, AO rating, no Play billing — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Online crane games may be listed globally since July 12, 2023 subject to law and listed requirements — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Prediction markets: apps offering real-money prediction-market functions had to enrol in the global pilot and meet its requirements by June 1, 2026 or face enforcement incl. removal — [Policy announcement April 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16926792)

**Health Content and Services**
- "All developers must complete the Health apps declaration form" in Play Console (App content) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Declaration categories: Health and fitness (fitness, nutrition, wellness, sleep, stress); Medical (provider apps, EHR, telehealth, symptom checkers, public-health apps, Software as a Medical Device); Human subjects research — [Health apps declaration help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13996367)
- Privacy policy must be linked in Play Console and in-app, on an active, public, non-geofenced URL (no PDFs), non-editable; apps accessing health data without being primarily health apps are still in scope — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- IF the app relies on external hardware (e.g. glucose monitor) THEN disclose the hardware requirement in the description; IF it uses device sensors for a health function THEN state device compatibility (oximetry apps relying only on device sensors must list supported models) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- IF regulated medical device THEN declare it, receive a "Medical Device" label, and provide proof of approval/clearance/certification on request. IF other health app THEN the description must carry a clear disclaimer that the app is "not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition", and the app must remind users to consult a healthcare professional — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- October 30, 2025: Health and Medical Functionalities policy incorporated elements of EU MDCG guidance and added the "Medical Device" label for EU-certified medical device apps — [Policy announcement October 30, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16550159)
- Health/medical claims must disclose purpose and intended use, claimed benefits, basis for claims, intended users, risks and required disclaimers in plain language — [Health apps declaration help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13996367)
- Government-affiliated health apps submit proof via the Advance Notice Form; contact-tracing/health-status apps select "Disease Prevention and Public Health"; human-subjects research apps need informed consent and IRB/ethics approval unless exempt — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Prescription drugs: no facilitating sale without a prescription. Unapproved substances: no promoting/selling regardless of claimed legality (ephedra; hCG for weight loss; supplements with active pharmaceutical ingredients; claims of equivalence to prescription drugs; products under regulatory warning). Health misinformation: no claims contradicting medical consensus or causing harm (vaccines alter DNA; harmful unapproved treatments; conversion therapy; "cures cancer"). Regulated clinical services must not use Play billing — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- April 15, 2026 reminder: sensitive health data cannot be used to determine employment or insurance eligibility or for unauthorised social sharing — [Policy announcement April 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16926792)

**Blockchain-based content**
- Crypto exchanges and software wallets: buying, holding or exchanging cryptocurrency must go through certified services in regulated jurisdictions; do not publish where prohibited; Google may request licensing documentation — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Exchange licensing by jurisdiction: Bahrain (CBB Crypto-Asset Services licence); Canada (FINTRAC MSB registration); Hong Kong (SFC Type 1 + Type 7 licences, or VATP licence); Indonesia (OJK Digital Financial Asset Trading Provider licence); Israel (CMISA Financial Services Provider licence or banking corporation); Japan (FSA crypto-asset exchange service provider registration); Philippines (BSP certificate of authority as Money Services Business); South Africa (FSCA registration); South Korea (KoFIU VASP report); Thailand (SEC Digital Asset Business licence); UAE (FSRA, VARA, DFSA or CMA licence); UK (FCA registration); US (FinCEN MSB + state money transmitter, or chartered bank); EU 27 states (MiCA CASP authorisation); Liechtenstein, Iceland, Norway added July 2026 — [Cryptocurrency exchanges and software wallets requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16329703)
- "Additional Options" (added October 2026) for developers without a local licence: (1) app is a non-custodial software wallet; (2) app does not offer buying/holding/exchanging in that country and is geo-restricted; (3) no local licence required or a statutory exemption applies, with a country-specific Full Description disclosing provider and regulatory status plus either a licence in another listed jurisdiction or a publicly traded parent (ISIN) — [Cryptocurrency exchanges and software wallets requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16329703)
- Process: declare "Cryptocurrency wallet" and/or "Cryptocurrency exchange" in the Financial features declaration, then complete location-specific forms or remove those countries — [Cryptocurrency exchanges and software wallets requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16329703)
- Cryptomining on-device is prohibited; remotely managing mining is allowed. Apps selling or letting users earn tokenized digital assets must declare it, label such in-app products, and may not promote or glamorise potential earnings. NFT gamification: no paying for a chance at an NFT of unknown value; purchased NFTs must be used in-game; no wagering NFTs. Violations: NFT bundles without disclosed contents/values; pay-to-play social casino games rewarding NFTs — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**News & Magazines**
- In scope: apps that declare themselves News or Magazine, or are in that category and describe themselves as "news" or "magazine" in title, icon, developer name or description. Must: self-declare in Play Console; provide source of articles (original publisher or author); update content regularly (no static content); provide clear, up-to-date contact information; identify publishing sources of third-party content (aggregators); provide an in-app content preview before purchase when subscription is required; affiliate marketing or ad revenue may not be the primary purpose — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346); section page: [News & Magazines](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9935326?hl=en)
- Help page states news apps providing current events are expected to have regularly updated content not more than 30 days old; the declaration covers contact details, parent entity type and aggregator status, one declaration per in-scope app (from search-result excerpt, page not fetched in full) — [Requirements for news and magazine apps](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10523915?hl=en)
- Declaration deadlines: August 27, 2025 for the expanded News & Magazines declaration — [Policy announcement July 10, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16296680); reminder that undeclared apps would be removed after May 27, 2026 — [Policy announcement April 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16926792)

**VPN apps**
- VpnService may be used only by apps with VPN as core functionality; exceptions: parental control/enterprise management, app usage tracking, device security (anti-virus, MDM, firewall), network tools (remote access), web browsers, carrier apps. Prohibited: collecting personal/sensitive data without prominent disclosure and consent; redirecting or manipulating other apps' traffic for monetisation. Required: document VpnService use in the Play listing; encrypt data from device to tunnel endpoint; comply with Ad Fraud, Permissions and Malware policies — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Apps approved to use VpnService require an Organization developer account — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Government apps**
- Organization account required for government apps; Government apps declaration mandatory since January 31, 2023; non-affiliated apps need source links and a non-affiliation statement (see Key Question 3) — [Requirements for apps that communicate government information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9514050?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Checklist triggers: any lending/credit wording → Finance category, APR/tenure/example disclosures, banned-permission scan, country licence upload; any health claim or health sensor use → declaration, disclaimer string in description, device-compatibility note; "news" in title or description → News declaration plus source attribution and contact page; VpnService in manifest → VPN-core test, listing disclosure, Organization account.
- A personal (non-Organization) developer account cannot publish finance, health/medical, VPN or government apps under the current Play Console Requirements, which is a hard blocker for small developers independent of app content.
- The Pakistan "one DLA per NBFC" rule and SECP approval requirement make Pakistan the strictest listed market for multi-app loan publishers.

### Gaps

- The news "30 days" freshness standard comes from a search excerpt of answer/10523915, not a full fetch; verify before relying on it.

## Key Question 6: What do the User Generated Content and AI-Generated Content policies require in-app?

### Takeaway
UGC apps need terms acceptance before posting, defined prohibited content, ongoing moderation, and in-app report/block tools scaled to the type of UGC; AI apps must prevent restricted and deceptive outputs and provide an in-app way to report or flag offensive AI output without leaving the app. Social, dating, anonymous-chat and random-chat apps additionally need Child Safety Standards self-certification and (for the last three plus dating) blocking of minors.

### Cited Findings

**User Generated Content**
- Definition: content users contribute that is visible to or accessible by at least some of the app's users; applies also to specialised browsers/clients that direct users to a UGC platform — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Required "robust, effective, and ongoing" moderation: (1) users must accept the app's terms of use and/or user policy before creating or uploading UGC; (2) terms must define and prohibit objectionable content and behaviours consistent with Play policies; (3) moderation reasonable for the UGC type, including an in-app system to report and block objectionable UGC and users, and action against them where appropriate — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- By app type: specified-user communities (single school/company, verified or offline-registered) → in-app reporting of content and users; 1:1 interaction (direct messaging, tagging, mentioning) → in-app blocking of users; publicly accessible UGC (social networking, blogging) → in-app report and block for both users and content; AR apps → moderation and reporting covering objectionable AR content and sensitive AR anchoring locations (military bases, private property) — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- In-app monetisation must include safeguards so it does not encourage objectionable user behaviour — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Incidental sexual content allowed only IF all hold: app is primarily non-sexual and does not promote/recommend it; content hidden by default behind filters requiring **at least two user actions** to fully disable; children explicitly barred by an age screen (e.g. neutral age screen) or other lawful system; content-rating questionnaire answered accurately regarding UGC. Illegal sexual content and child-endangerment content are never incidental — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Removal criteria: apps whose primary purpose is featuring objectionable UGC, or that become used primarily for or gain a reputation for hosting it — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Violation examples: promoting sexually explicit UGC incl. paid features that mainly encourage sharing objectionable content; insufficient safeguards against threats, harassment or bullying, particularly toward minors; posts/comments/photos primarily intended to harass or single out a person; continually failing to address user complaints about objectionable content — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**AI-Generated Content**
- Definition: content created by generative AI models in response to user prompts, e.g. text-to-text conversational chatbots where interacting with the chatbot is a central feature, and images or videos generated from text, image or voice prompts — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Requirements: comply with all existing policies; prohibit and prevent generation of Restricted Content, including content facilitating child exploitation/abuse, and content enabling deceptive behaviour — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- In-app reporting: apps must contain in-app features that let users report or flag offensive AI-generated content to developers without needing to exit the app; developers should use these reports to inform content filtering and moderation — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Best-practices article (linked from policy since July 10, 2025): developers are responsible for ensuring generative AI apps do not produce offensive content; adopt content safeguards; test in line with Google's Secure AI Framework (SAIF) and the OWASP GenAI Red Teaming Guide; use input/output filters and classifiers; consult model providers' safety resources — [Best practices for generative AI apps](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16353813)
- July 15, 2026 clarification (User Data): requirements also cover third-party AI integrations; the developer stays responsible for limited use, disclosure and consent — [Policy announcement July 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731)
- October 29, 2026: new AppFunctions API policy — AppFunctions are "strictly prohibited from deceiving or manipulating AI agents", bypassing security controls or interfering with other apps; deadline April 14, 2027 — [Policy Deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780)
- October 30, 2025: Accessibility API may not be used to let an app autonomously initiate, plan and execute actions or decisions — [Policy announcement October 30, 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16550159)
- Related absolute prohibitions that AI apps commonly hit: apps claiming to undress people or see through clothing; deepfake non-consensual sexual content; apps whose sole purpose is creating misleading media; generating IDs, passports, diplomas — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

**Social / dating / chat overlays**
- Child Safety Standards self-certification (published CSAE prohibition, in-app reporting, CSAM process, legal compliance, named contact) applies to Anonymous Chat, Random Chat, Social and Dating apps — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)
- Dating/matchmaking core apps, random-chat core apps and anonymous-communication core apps must block minors via Play Console — [DPP](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18265346)

### Inferences
- UI checks: (a) ToS/community-guidelines acceptance screen before first post/upload; (b) a "Report" control on every piece of public content and every profile; (c) a "Block user" control wherever DMs, mentions or tagging exist; (d) for any generative feature, a flag/report control attached to each AI output inside the app (an email link that leaves the app would not satisfy "without needing to exit the app"); (e) a moderation backend that actually acts on reports.
- An app that embeds a third-party LLM chat as a central feature is in scope of the AI policy even if the model is not the developer's own; the developer also inherits User Data duties for data sent to that provider under the July 2026 clarification.
- Comments, reviews, public profiles, shared playlists or marketplace listings all count as UGC under the definition, so small apps with a comment box need report/block tooling.

### Gaps
- The current AI-Generated Content section, as returned by the fetch tool, showed no list of AI-specific violation examples or explicit out-of-scope statement. Earlier versions of the policy are widely reported to have listed examples (e.g. non-consensual deepfake sexual material, voice/video for scams, election misinformation, sexually gratifying generative apps, malicious code generation) and exclusions (apps merely hosting AI content, summarising non-AI content, productivity apps using AI). Whether these remain in the October 29, 2026 text could not be confirmed and should be verified on the live page.
- No Google-stated service levels exist for how fast UGC/AI reports must be actioned; none found.
- Requirements specific to AI companion/character chat apps or AI age-rating were not found as a distinct policy.
- The detailed July 15, 2026 "new requirements and restrictions" for anonymous/random chat apps beyond minor-blocking and Child Safety Standards were not retrieved verbatim.
