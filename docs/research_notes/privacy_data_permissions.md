# Google Play Privacy, User Data and Permissions Requirements (state as of 14 November 2026)

Reading notes for the report writer: every finding below was read from the cited page on 2026-11-14 unless a date is given. Pages were read through a fetch tool that summarises long pages, so short quoted phrases are as relayed by that tool and several Help pages were truncated; treat wording as close paraphrase, not certified verbatim. "PC" = Play Console. Upcoming (2027) rules are labelled UPCOMING so they are not mistaken for rules already enforced.

## 1. What does the User Data policy require (privacy policy, prominent disclosure and consent, personal/sensitive data handling, data sale)?

### Takeaway
Every app needs a privacy policy linked both in Play Console and inside the app, must limit personal and sensitive data to purposes users reasonably expect, must never sell that data, and must show an in-app prominent disclosure followed by affirmative consent before any collection that users would not expect (and before the runtime permission prompt). Since the 15 July 2026 announcement Google states the same rules explicitly cover third-party AI integrations.

### Cited Findings

Privacy policy
- A privacy policy is required in the designated Play Console field AND within the app itself; apps that access no personal or sensitive data still need one. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- The policy must cover: developer information and a privacy point of contact or inquiry mechanism; the types of personal and sensitive data accessed, collected, used and shared, and the parties it is shared with; secure data handling procedures; data retention and deletion policy; and it must be clearly labelled as a privacy policy. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- The entity (developer/company) named on the store listing must appear in the privacy policy, or the app must be named in it. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- The policy must be on an active, publicly accessible, non-geofenced URL, must not be a PDF, and must be non-editable. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- Anti-virus / security apps must post a privacy policy that explains what data is collected, how it is used and with whom it is shared. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- If data is retained after account deletion for security, fraud prevention or regulatory reasons, that retention must be disclosed in the privacy policy. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)

Personal and sensitive user data
- Definition includes: personally identifiable information, financial and payment information, authentication information, phonebook/contacts, device location, SMS and call related data, health data and Health Connect data, inventory of other apps on the device, microphone, camera, and other sensitive device or usage data. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- Handling rules: limit access, collection, use and sharing to app functionality and policy-conforming purposes the user reasonably expects; ads use must follow the Ads policy; transfers are allowed to service providers or for legal reasons (valid governmental request, applicable law, merger/acquisition with legally adequate notice); handle data securely including modern cryptography in transit (e.g. HTTPS); use a runtime permission request before accessing data gated by Android permissions. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- Sale prohibited: personal and sensitive user data may not be sold; "sale" means exchange or transfer to a third party for monetary consideration; a user-initiated transfer (for example the user sending a file to a third party through an app feature) is not a sale. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- Financial/payment information and government ID numbers must never be publicly disclosed; non-public phonebook/contact information must not be published or disclosed without authorisation. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- Third-party code: developers must ensure SDKs (and, as clarified in 2026, AI integrations) in the app comply; SDK providers must not sell personal and sensitive data from the app; the developer remains responsible. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- 15 July 2026 announcement wording: "We're clarifying that our User Data requirements also apply to third-party AI integrations." Developers remain responsible for limited use, disclosure and consent. Compliance window "at least 30 days from July 15, 2026". — [Policy announcement: July 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731)

Prominent disclosure and consent (exact format rules)
- Trigger: required when the app's access, collection, use or sharing of personal and sensitive data may not be within the reasonable expectation of the user (for example collection in the background). — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- Disclosure MUST: be within the app itself (not only in the store description or a website); be displayed in normal usage of the app without requiring navigation into a menu or settings; describe the data being accessed or collected; explain how it will be used and/or shared; NOT be placed only in a privacy policy or terms of service; NOT be bundled with other disclosures unrelated to personal and sensitive data collection. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- Consent MUST: immediately follow the disclosure and come before any runtime permission request; be presented clearly and unambiguously; require affirmative user action (tap to accept, tick a checkbox); NOT treat navigating away (tap away, back or home button) as consent; NOT use auto-dismissing or expiring messages; be granted before any collection or access begins. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- Recommended disclosure sentence format: "[This app] collects/transmits/syncs/stores [type of data] to enable ["feature"], [in what scenario]." — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- SDK evidence rule: where an SDK collects personal and sensitive data by default, the developer must provide evidence of prominent disclosure and consent compliance within 2 weeks of a Google Play request (or a longer period if Google specifies one). — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311); [SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374)
- Listed violation examples: collecting device location without a prominent disclosure explaining the feature and any background use; requesting the runtime permission before showing the disclosure; treating installed-app inventory, contacts or screen recordings as non-sensitive data; using restricted permissions in the background for tracking, research or marketing without adequate disclosure and consent; SDKs collecting sensitive data outside these requirements. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)

Device identifiers (READ_PHONE_STATE and similar)
- Persistent device identifiers (IMEI, IMSI, SIM Serial #, etc.) may not be linked to other personal and sensitive user data or to resettable device identifiers, EXCEPT for (a) telephony linked to a SIM identity (e.g. Wi-Fi calling linked to a carrier account) and (b) enterprise device management apps using device owner mode; these uses must be prominently disclosed. SDKs that cannot be made compliant must be removed. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- Listed SDK violation example: transmitting IMEI obtained via the read_phone_state permission without user consent; linking the Android Advertising ID to persistent identifiers or to email for ads/analytics. — [SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374)
- App Set ID: for essential non-ads uses (analytics, fraud prevention); must not be used for ads personalisation or ads measurement; must not be linked to the advertising ID or to personal data for advertising; its collection must be disclosed in the privacy policy, with legally valid consent where required. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)

Cross-border frameworks
- For personal information originating in the EEA, UK or Switzerland received via Google, developers must comply with applicable law, use it only consistently with consent, give protection equivalent to the Data Privacy Framework principles, monitor compliance, and notify data-protection-office@google.com and stop processing (or remediate) if they cannot comply. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)

### Inferences
- Pre-submission checks derivable from this section: (1) privacy policy URL present in PC and reachable without login/geofence, HTML not PDF; (2) same policy reachable from inside the app; (3) policy text names the developer entity or the app, lists data types, sharing recipients, retention/deletion and a contact; (4) every unexpected or background collection has an in-app disclosure screen that precedes the OS permission dialog and has an explicit accept control; (5) no code path treats dismissing the disclosure as consent; (6) no IMEI/IMSI/serial joined with advertising ID or account data; (7) any outbound call that sends user content to an external AI/model API is listed in the privacy policy and Data safety form and gated by consent where it is unexpected.
- Item (7) is partly inference: Google's announcement says the User Data requirements "also apply" to AI integrations; the specific steps (list the model provider as a recipient, consent before transfer) come from a secondary article — [dev.to summary, July 2026](https://dev.to/mr_manushukla/google-plays-july-2026-policy-update-5-changes-to-ship-before-the-31-august-deadline-3bie) — and are a reasonable reading rather than Google's own checklist.

### Gaps
- The User Data policy page was truncated by the fetch tool; no effective date appears on it (footer only shows 2026). The exact current paragraph on AI integrations inside the policy body was not retrieved; only the announcement wording was.
- No Google page was found that specifies how the in-app privacy policy link must be surfaced (e.g. settings vs. onboarding); the policy only says "within the app itself".

## 2. Data safety form: what must be declared, taxonomy, SDK counting, mismatch rejections and detection

### Takeaway
Every app on a closed, open or production track must complete the Data safety form covering all data the app and its SDKs transmit off-device, by data type and purpose, plus security practices and data deletion answers; the form must match the privacy policy and actual behaviour. Google says its review does not verify accuracy, but it may detect discrepancies between the declaration and app behaviour and block updates or remove the app.

### Cited Findings

Who and when
- Required for every app on Google Play including closed testing, open testing and production, and preloaded apps updated via Play; apps that collect no data still must complete it and supply a privacy policy link. One form covers the whole package (all versions currently distributed, worldwide). — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- Exempt: apps active only on internal testing tracks; system services (on application); private apps; old artifacts with effective target SDK below 21 where 90% or more of the active install base is on target SDK 21+. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- The User Data policy itself requires an accurate, up-to-date Data safety section consistent with the privacy policy, covering collection by SDKs. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- Since 20 July 2022 all apps need an accurate form (grace period for publishing with unresolved issues ended); internal-testing exemption effective 24 October 2022. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)

Definitions
- "Collected" = transmitted off the user's device, including by SDKs/libraries and from a webview whose content the app controls; pseudonymous data counts. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- "Shared" = transferred to a third party, including server-to-server transfers, on-device transfer to another app, and transfers via SDKs or app-controlled webviews. Third party = any organisation other than the first party (the publisher) or its service providers. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- Not collection: data processed only on-device and never sent off it; end-to-end encrypted data unreadable by the developer; ephemeral processing (held only in memory, discarded after the request) — ephemeral data is still entered in the form but not shown on the listing, and building advertising or user profiles never qualifies as ephemeral. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- Not sharing: transfer to a service provider processing on the developer's behalf; transfer for legal purposes; user-initiated transfer the user reasonably expects, or transfer after a prominent in-app disclosure and consent meeting the User Data policy; fully anonymised data. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- Payment data handled wholly by a payment service (e.g. Play Billing, PayPal) that the app never accesses need not be declared, but purchase history may; data a user uploads straight to their own cloud storage that the app never accesses need not be declared. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)

Data types taxonomy
- Location: approximate (area >= 3 sq km), precise (< 3 sq km); IP-derived location counts. Personal info: name, email address, user IDs, address, phone number, race and ethnicity, political or religious beliefs, sexual orientation, other info. Financial info: user payment info, purchase history, credit score, other financial info. Health and fitness: health info, fitness info. Messages: emails, SMS or MMS, other in-app messages. Photos and videos: photos, videos. Audio files: voice or sound recordings, music files, other audio files. Files and docs. Calendar: calendar events. Contacts. App activity: app interactions, in-app search history, installed apps, other user-generated content, other actions. Web browsing: web browsing history. App info and performance: crash logs, diagnostics, other app performance data. Device or other IDs. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- Purposes taxonomy: App functionality; Analytics; Developer communications; Advertising or marketing; Fraud prevention, security, and compliance; Personalization; Account management. Each data type needs its collection purposes and sharing purposes, and whether collection is required or optional (if any version or user requires it, declare "required"). — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- 15 July 2026: Google added guidance on precise vs approximate location disclosures in the Data safety section. — [Policy announcement: July 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731)

SDKs
- Data collected or shared by third-party SDKs/libraries counts toward the app's declaration whether it goes to the developer or a third party; the developer is responsible; Google points to the Google Play SDK Index and SDK providers' published Data safety guidance. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- Example of provider guidance: Play Age Signals client library states "No data is collected" by the library. — [Play Age Signals overview](https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/overview)

Security practices and deletion answers
- "Encrypted in transit" may be declared only if ALL user data collected by the app and its SDKs is encrypted in transit. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- "Deletion request mechanism" may be declared if users can request deletion or data is automatically deleted/anonymised within 90 days. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- Data deletion questions (account creation yes/no, deletion URL) live inside the Data safety form; errors in them can cause new submissions and updates to be rejected. — [Account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)
- Optional badges: Independent security review (MASA, OWASP MASVS, Google-authorised lab, developer-funded); "Committed to follow the Play Families Policy"; UPI badge (India). The security review does not verify Data safety accuracy. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)

Mismatch / enforcement / detection
- Google reviews the form as part of app review, but "the review does not verify the accuracy" of declarations; only the developer has the information. Google may detect discrepancies between declarations and app behaviour and take enforcement action; non-compliant apps may have updates blocked or be removed. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- The Data safety section is distinct from the permissions list: permissions list is derived from the manifest, Data safety reports actual collection and sharing. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- Play Console flags SDK versions using the dependencies metadata in the app bundle, and may direct developers to "ensure your SDK usage complies with the user data policies". — [Using third-party SDKs in your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10358880)
- Form review can take up to 7 days or longer; CSV export/import is supported (import overwrites answers). — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)

### Inferences
- Mismatch triggers that follow directly from the definitions: an SDK (ads, analytics, crash reporting, attribution) transmitting device IDs, advertising ID, diagnostics or location that is not declared; declaring "encrypted in transit" while any endpoint is plain HTTP; declaring "no data collected" while the manifest holds AD_ID or location permissions and network traffic carries those values; declaring optional collection that is actually mandatory; account creation present but data deletion questions answered "no account"; privacy policy listing data types absent from the form (the policy requires consistency).
- A practical pre-submission check is therefore a three-way diff: manifest permissions + SDK list vs. Data safety answers vs. privacy policy text.

### Gaps
- Google does not publish how it detects mismatches (static analysis, traffic inspection, SDK fingerprints). The only official statements found are "may detect discrepancies" and that SDK detection uses the bundle's dependencies file. Any more specific mechanism claimed in blogs is unconfirmed.
- No official list of the most common "Data safety form mismatch / Invalid Data safety form" rejection causes was found; the list under Inferences is derived, not quoted.
- The new July 2026 location-disclosure guidance text itself was not retrieved (announcement page truncated).

## 3. Account deletion requirement

### Takeaway
Any app that lets users create an account (in-app or by sending them to an external sign-up flow) must offer a discoverable in-app path to start account deletion AND a working web link for deletion that is entered in Play Console's Data deletion questions; deleting the account must delete associated data, and deactivation or freezing does not count.

### Cited Findings
- Scope: applies if the app allows account creation inside the app or directs users to an account creation flow outside the app; having features usable without an account does not exempt it. — [Account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)
- Exceptions: permanently private apps and enterprise device management apps are exempt; accounts created and operated entirely offline are not "app accounts"; non-mobile surfaces (Android TV, Wear OS, web) need no in-app option but still need a discoverable web route and the URL in PC. — [Account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)
- In-app path: users must be able to initiate deletion from within the app (e.g. account settings); path should be prominent and intuitive; a complete in-app flow is recommended but the app may instead link out to the web deletion resource. — [Account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)
- Web link: mandatory for all apps with account creation even when in-app deletion exists (covers users who uninstalled); must be functional, relevant to the app, and reference the app or developer name as shown on the store listing; the request route must be prominent (link, form or support email); the user must not have to reinstall the app; prerequisite steps (e.g. cancelling a subscription) must be explained; if a privacy policy page is reused, the deletion section must be prominent (e.g. anchor link). — [Account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)
- Policy text: deletion must remove the associated user data; temporary deactivation, disabling or "freezing" does not qualify; data retained for security, fraud prevention or regulatory compliance must be disclosed in the privacy policy. — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- "Associated data" includes all data declared as collected in the Data safety section; data held by service providers should also be deleted on request; a partial "delete some data without deleting account" option may be offered and declared. — [Account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)
- PC declaration: all developers must answer the Data deletion questions in the Data safety form (App content page); apps with account creation must state that they offer deletion and supply the web URL; answers surface on the store listing as a data deletion badge/area. — [Account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)
- Timeline (historical): announced April 2023; questions due 7 December 2023; extension available to 31 May 2024; after 31 May 2024 non-compliant apps face enforcement including removal. — [Account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)
- Speed: no fixed number of days; developers must tell users what to expect and complete requests reasonably quickly, subject to local law. — [Account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)

### Inferences
- Concrete checks: (1) app has sign-up or login -> settings screen contains "Delete account"; (2) PC Data deletion answer = account creation yes + URL; (3) URL loads without login wall, names the app/developer, and shows how to request deletion; (4) privacy policy lists what is retained after deletion and why; (5) backend actually deletes rather than flags inactive.
- Social sign-in (Google/Facebook) that creates a server-side user record is an "account" for this rule; this follows from the scope wording but is not stated in those exact words.

### Gaps
- Whether a mailto: link alone satisfies the "web link" field in every review is not stated; the page lists a support email as one acceptable request route on the web resource.

## 4. Permissions policy: restricted and sensitive permissions and the rule for each

### Takeaway
Sensitive permissions must be necessary for core functionality that is promoted in the store listing, requested in context, and never used to sell data; a defined set (SMS/Call Log, background location, all files access, photo/video, QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES, REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES, Accessibility, VPN, exact alarm, full-screen intent, foreground service types, Health Connect/health permissions) additionally needs an approved Play Console declaration, or the permission must be removed from the manifest. Further restrictions on contacts, fine location, call-log verification, local network and MANAGE_CONTACTS are scheduled for 2027.

### Cited Findings

General rules
- Permissions and APIs that access sensitive information must be necessary for current core functionality promoted in the Play listing; request incrementally and in context; obtain new consent for new purposes; never sell the data or share it for a purpose facilitating sale; honour denial of a restricted permission and offer a reasonable alternative without manipulating the user. — [Permissions and APIs that Access Sensitive Information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170)

SMS and Call Log detail
- App must be actively registered as default SMS, Phone or Assistant handler before prompting for these permissions and must stop using them when no longer default. Default SMS handler may hold all six SMS permissions; default Phone handler SEND_SMS, PROCESS_OUTGOING_CALLS, READ_CALL_LOG, WRITE_CALL_LOG; default Assistant the SMS set plus READ_CALL_LOG. — [SMS/Call Log help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820)
- Exceptions for non-default apps (only when no alternative exists, subject to review): account verification via phone call (READ_CALL_LOG; removed 27 Jan 2027); anti-smishing (needs a track record of significant user protection); backup and restore (must be able to restore to the original default handler); caller ID / spam detection and blocking; connected device companion; cross-device sync; device automation; enterprise archive / CRM / device management (corporate login required); government or MNO quality-of-service reporting; in-vehicle hands-free / projected display; emergency SMS alerts (SEND_SMS); proxy calls; SMS cell broadcast (RECEIVE_SMS); SMS-based financial transactions; call-based banking/brokerage authentication; SMS-based money management (read-only SMS set); system services with SYSTEM_UI_INTELLIGENCE role. — [SMS/Call Log help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820)
- Invalid uses: OTP/account verification via broad SMS or call-log access, content sharing or invites, contact prioritisation by non-default apps, social graph/personality profiling, call recording, performance boosting, storage management, family locating, smart keyboards, launchers/wallpapers showing SMS, translation/TTS of SMS, research, any transfer resulting in sale (including via SDKs). — [SMS/Call Log help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820)
- Alternatives Google names: Digital Credentials API (recommended) and SMS Retriever API, SMS User Consent API, manual code entry, Incoming Call Retriever API; SMS intent; share intent; dial intent (no CALL_PHONE needed). — [SMS/Call Log help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820)
- Legacy APK exception: version codes declared in the form's APK Exceptions field, published before 1 Jan 2019, compliant APKs served to API 26+, affected APKs a low single-digit percentage of installs. — [SMS/Call Log help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820)
- UPCOMING 27 Jan 2027: READ_CALL_LOG can no longer be used for phone-call account verification. — [Policy Deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780); announced in [Policy announcement: July 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731)

Location detail
- Minimum scope (coarse over fine, foreground over background); never request location solely for advertising or analytics; stop access when no longer needed; foreground-service location only as a continuation of an in-app user-initiated action and terminated immediately after that action completes; child-directed apps must follow Families policy. — [Permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170)
- Background location must be important to core functionality; evaluated on user benefit (physical safety, perceived safety, health/fitness = significant; ads, analytics, personalisation, entertainment, convenience = minimal). Features Google says can normally be done in foreground (higher rejection risk): nearby friends only while app open, local content personalisation, regional DRM, delivery tracking for the customer (not the driver), turn-by-turn navigation without passive tracking, aggregated traffic/speed maps. — [Location help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)
- Background location prominent disclosure wording must: include the word "location"; state background use with one of "background", "when the app is closed", "always in use", "when the app is not in use"; list all features using background location; if used for ads, say so (e.g. "used to provide ads"). Recommended sentence: "[This app] collects location data to enable [feature], [feature], & [feature] even when the app is closed or not in use." It must show before the runtime prompt, in normal use; for a feature with no UI, on first app open. For this disclosure an explicit consent button is not required (user may dismiss). — [Location help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)
- UPCOMING 27 Jan 2027 (apps targeting Android 17 / API 37+): if precise location is needed only for one-time, user-initiated actions, the app "must use the Android location button" and declare the `onlyForLocationButton` flag in its manifest; ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION allowed only if neither the button nor coarse location can support the feature, with a PC declaration justifying it. — [Policy preview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16909972); [Android Developers Blog, April 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/04/giving-users-clearer-choice-and-everyone-a-safer-more-trusted-app-ecosystem.html)
- 2026 change already announced: geofencing removed as an approved foreground-service use case (use the Geofence API); deadline 27 Jan 2027. — [Policy announcement: April 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16926792); [Policy Deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780)

All files access
- Applies to apps targeting Android 11 (API 30)+ declaring MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE; does not affect READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE. Permitted: file management, backup and restore, anti-virus, document management (must justify why SAF/app storage insufficient), on-device file search, disk/file encryption and locking, device migration/phone transfer. Temporary exceptions possible where no alternative exists. Invalid: accessing media files (use MediaStore), user-picked single files (use SAF). Users must be prompted to enable "All files access" under Special app access. — [All files access help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10467955); [Permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170)

Photo and video
- "All user Photos are personal and sensitive data." Apps targeting API 33+ may request READ_MEDIA_IMAGES / READ_MEDIA_VIDEO only if system pickers (Android Photo Picker) are not sufficient for core functionality; example of qualifying app: gallery apps managing all photos/videos; apps with their own custom picker do not qualify automatically. Apps without a supported core use case must remove both permissions. Policy introduced October 2023; full compliance mandatory from 28 May 2025 (including developers who had an extension); non-compliant apps subject to removal. Private and enterprise device management apps exempt. — [Photo and Video help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14115180); [Permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241)
- Picker integration: androidx.activity 1.7.0+; picker available on Android 11+ via Google System Updates, with backport to Android 4.4-10 via Google Play services. — [Photo and Video help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14115180)
- April 2026: policy text "clarified for developer ease", enforcement unchanged. — [Policy announcement: April 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16926792)

QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES
- Permitted when core purpose requires discovering any installed app for awareness or interoperability: device search, antivirus, file managers, browsers. Exceptions: real-money gambling apps needing it for geofencing standards (temporary); apps whose verifiable core purpose is financially regulated transactions (banking, digital wallets) for security only; personal loan apps are NOT eligible. Invalid: P2P sharing unless that is the core purpose, acquiring data for sale, sharing inventory for analytics/ads monetisation, any case where targeted `<queries>` would work. Installed-app inventory is personal and sensitive data. — [Package visibility help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10158779)

REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES
- Core functionality must include sending or receiving app packages AND enabling user-initiated installation. Permitted: web browsing/search, communication services with attachments, file sharing/transfer/management, enterprise device management, backup and restore, device migration; the policy page also lists companion apps syncing to wearables/IoT. Not for self-update outside Play's update mechanism, silent installs or bundled APKs (device management excepted). Enforced since 11 July 2022. — [REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12085295); [Permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170)

Accessibility API
- Prohibited: changing user settings without permission, preventing users from disabling/uninstalling apps (unless authorised by parent or enterprise admin), working around Android privacy/security controls, deceptive UI changes, remote call audio recording, and apps that autonomously initiate, plan and execute actions or decisions (deterministic rule-based automation from a static human-defined script is not prohibited). Use must be documented in the Play listing. — [Policy preview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16909972); [Accessibility help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)
- isAccessibilityTool=true only for apps whose primary purpose is supporting people with disabilities (screen readers, switch/voice input, braille access, cognitive aids). NOT eligible: antivirus, automation tools, assistants, monitoring apps, cleaners, password managers, launchers. Tools are exempt from the prominent disclosure and consent requirement; all other apps need a separate in-app disclosure with affirmative consent. — [Accessibility help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)

VPN Service
- Only apps with VPN as core functionality, or needing a remote server for: parental control and enterprise management, app usage tracking, device security (anti-virus, MDM, firewall), network tools (remote access), web browsing, carrier telephony/connectivity. Must not collect personal and sensitive data without prominent disclosure and consent, must not redirect or manipulate other apps' traffic for monetisation, must document VPN use in the listing and encrypt data from device to tunnel endpoint. Declaration available since 1 Nov 2022; submission deadline was 31 Aug 2023. — [VpnService help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12564964)

Exact alarm and full-screen intent
- USE_EXACT_ALARM only if "core, user facing functionality requires precisely-timed actions": "The app is an alarm or timer app" or "a calendar app that shows event notifications"; others should evaluate SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM; PC declaration required. — [Permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16585319)
- USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT: special app access for apps targeting Android 14+; pre-granted only for core functionality of setting an alarm or receiving phone/video calls; others must request it from the user and degrade gracefully; not for ads or disruptive notifications. Declaration available April 2024, restrictions effective 31 May 2024, default-grant limited to calling and alarm apps from 22 Jan 2025. — [FGS and FSI help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821); [Permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16585319)

Foreground services
- Apps targeting Android 14 (API 34)+ must declare a foreground service type for every foreground service plus the matching FOREGROUND_SERVICE_<TYPE> permission, and complete a PC declaration per type. Types: camera, connectedDevice, dataSync, health, location, mediaPlayback, mediaProjection, mediaProcessing, microphone, phoneCall, remoteMessaging, plus specialUse (limited cases) and systemExempted. Use must be user-perceptible (user initiated it or a clear notification); auto-playing media and fully automatic background backup do not qualify; Google recommends user-initiated data transfer jobs instead of dataSync for explicit transfers. Declaration available since November 2023. — [FGS and FSI help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)

Health Connect, body sensors, health permissions
- Body sensor data (heart rate, SpO2, skin temperature) is personal and sensitive; apps targeting Android 16 (API 36)+ must use granular android.permission.health.* permissions (e.g. READ_HEART_RATE) rather than BODY_SENSORS; approved uses: fitness and wellness tracking, medical/condition monitoring, approved health research, wearable companion features; no advertising, analytics or profiling on inferred health conditions. — [Permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16585319)
- Health Connect approved use cases: fitness and wellness, rewards, fitness coaching, corporate wellness, medical care, health research, games. Prohibited: sale or transfer for advertising, creditworthiness or lending; high-risk systems where failure could cause death or injury; headless apps (must have an identifiable icon); apps solely targeting children. Limited-use rules: use only for the visible use case; third-party transfer only with explicit consent or for security/legal/M&A; restricted human access. Requires minimum permissions, comprehensive privacy policy, PC declaration with detailed justification. — [Permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16585319)
- April 2026: guidelines updated for Android 16 granular permissions and new Health Connect data types (Menstrual Cycle Phases, Alcohol Consumption, Symptoms); sensitive health data may not be used to determine employment or insurance eligibility, or for unauthorised social sharing. — [Policy announcement: April 15, 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16926792)
- Every published app (including "no health features") must complete the Health apps declaration; required since 31 Aug 2024; system services and private apps exempt. — [Health apps declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14738291)

Advertising ID
- Apps targeting Android 13+ must declare `com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID` to read the advertising ID; SDKs such as play-services-ads add it through manifest merge even if the app does not declare it; enforced for all devices from 1 April 2022. When a user deletes the advertising ID the API returns zeros. Advertising must use the advertising ID (when available) rather than other device identifiers; persistent IDs only for non-advertising purposes with a privacy policy. — [Advertising ID help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6048248)
- Advertising ID must not be linked to persistent identifiers or to personal data such as email for ads/analytics. — [SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374)

Age Signals API
- Data from the Age Signals API may be used "for the sole purpose" of complying with legal/regulatory obligations such as age-appropriate experiences; prohibited: advertising, marketing, personalisation, analytics, user profiling, business intelligence, and selling/sharing/transferring to third parties except as strictly required by law. — [Permissions policy, Age Signals section](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16585319#age_signals)

AppFunctions (UPCOMING 14 April 2027)
- AppFunctions must accurately represent capabilities; no deceiving or manipulating AI agents, no hidden system instructions returned at runtime, no dictating rules for other apps' AppFunctions, no bypassing agent safety controls or interfering with other apps; input schemas limited to what is needed; data handling under User Data policy and Data safety. — [Policy preview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16909972)

### Inferences
- For a manifest scanner, the "must have declaration or be removed" set as of Nov 2026 is: SMS group, Call Log group, ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION, MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, READ_MEDIA_IMAGES/VIDEO (target 33+), QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES, REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES, accessibility service, VPN service, USE_EXACT_ALARM, USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT (target 34+), every foregroundServiceType (target 34+), any android.permission.health.* / BODY_SENSORS. A second "warn, 2027" set is READ_CONTACTS, ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION, READ_CALL_LOG for verification, geofencing via FGS, ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK, MANAGE_CONTACTS.
- Because SDK manifests merge into the app manifest, each of these checks must run on the merged manifest, not the source manifest (stated for AD_ID; the same merge behaviour applies to other permissions by Android build design).
- Camera, microphone, calendar, activity recognition and current READ_CONTACTS have no dedicated declaration form today; they are governed by the general core-functionality, runtime-request and prominent-disclosure rules.

### Gaps
- Play Console "Advertising ID" declaration (App content): the Advertising ID help page was truncated and did not show this section. From prior knowledge (NOT confirmed from an official page in this session): apps targeting Android 13+ must answer whether they use the advertising ID and for what purposes, and a release is blocked if the answer is "No" while the merged manifest contains AD_ID. Verify at https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6048248 before relying on it.
- No dedicated Help article for the exact alarm declaration was found; the rule is cited from the policy text only.
- Whether QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES, All files access and REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES declarations require a video: their own pages do not say; the generic Permissions Declaration Form page lists a video demo as a step.
- requestLegacyExternalStorage handling is not covered on the All files access page.
- The complete current Contacts section of the policy (before the January 2027 change) was not retrieved in full.
- "Credential Manager" has no Play permission policy of its own that was found; the relevant link is that Google recommends the Digital Credentials API for phone number verification instead of SMS/call-log access.

## 5. For each permission needing a Play Console declaration: what the form asks, evidence, common rejection reasons

### Takeaway
The generic Permissions Declaration Form asks for the core-functionality use case, reviewer instructions, a demo video link and test credentials; location, accessibility, VPN, foreground service and health declarations have their own specific questions and video content rules. Google only publishes an explicit rejection-reason list for background location and Health Connect.

### Cited Findings

Generic Permissions Declaration Form (SMS/Call Log, All files, QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES, REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES and others)
- Shown during release when the bundle requests high-risk permissions not previously declared. Steps: (1) review detected permissions (remove unintended ones by uploading a new bundle); (2) select supported core-functionality use cases; (3) instructions for review; (4) video demo — required — YouTube preferred or cloud-storage link to MP4; (5) app access: if login is needed, supply test-only credentials and instructions; (6) multi-APK exception field (SMS/Call Log legacy APK version codes); (7) confirm accuracy. — [Declare permissions for your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9214102)
- A bundle needing a declaration on ANY track (internal, closed, open) raises an alert in App content and blocks publishing other changes (listing, pricing, distribution) until resolved; deactivate unused test tracks. Review "can take up to several weeks". For urgent releases remove the permission and resubmit. Publishing API uploads with unapproved permissions return an error; the form must be completed in the web UI. — [Declare permissions for your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9214102)
- Any change in use requires resubmitting; deceptive or undeclared use may lead to suspension or developer account termination; apps without an approved form may be removed. — [SMS/Call Log help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820); [All files access help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10467955); [Package visibility help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10158779)

Background location declaration
- Path: App content > Sensitive app permissions > Location permissions. Asks for ONE location-based feature, why it cannot work without background access and how it relates to the app's main purpose, plus a video link. Declaring multiple features causes rejection; approval applies to the whole app. — [Location help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)
- Video: 30 seconds or less recommended; must show the feature being triggered from the background, the prominent disclosure, and the runtime permission prompt, plus in-app steps to reach the feature; recorded on an Android device; YouTube preferred, Drive MP4 accepted; if no UI, say so and show effects. — [Location help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)
- Published rejection reasons: unclear feature description or multiple features; feature not verifiable in the app or video does not match; feature ineligible or achievable in foreground; ads as the only reason; missing/invalid privacy policy or link; disclosure missing, missing required content, or shown after the runtime prompt; missing or expired test credentials; in-app feature differs from declaration. — [Location help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)
- Bundles on all tracks including closed and open testing are reviewed; without approval updates may be blocked and the app removed. — [Location help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)

Accessibility declaration
- Accessibility tools: describe one core feature; choose disability types served (motor, vision, hearing, cognitive/learning, other); identify target users; short video showing app opening, service being enabled and used. — [Accessibility help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)
- Non-tools: choose why the API is needed (Data safety purpose list); state whether personal/sensitive data is collected or shared via accessibility and which types; video must show app opening, path to the disclosure and consent screen with full text legible, the consent flow including granting the permission, the non-consent flow and how to re-open the disclosure, and a core feature using accessibility. The service description string cannot replace the in-app disclosure. — [Accessibility help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)

VPN declaration
- Questions: is VPN the core functionality; which permitted category applies (choosing "none" requires detailed explanation or removal of VPN services from all artifacts on all tracks); video (<= 90 s) of the app opening and VPN in use; does the VPN collect or share data and which types; second video (<= 90 s) showing the prominent disclosure with consent and non-consent flows; does the app redirect/manipulate other apps' traffic for monetisation (yes with ad manipulation = rejection). Picking a category that does not match the app's core purpose can cause rejection. — [VpnService help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12564964)

Foreground service declaration
- Per type: description of the functionality; user impact if the task is deferred or interrupted; video link showing the user steps that trigger the feature; use case chosen from a preset list or "other". Type-specific video rules: connectedDevice must show in-app pairing (not system settings); dataSync must not show fully automatic/background backup. — [FGS and FSI help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)

Photo and video declaration
- Must explain why the Android Photo Picker or other system picker is insufficient for core functionality; apps with custom pickers must still justify. — [Photo and Video help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14115180)

Health apps / Health Connect declaration
- Health apps declaration: choose categories (Health and fitness; Medical; Medical device apps; Human subjects research; or "no health features"); regulated medical device apps may need proof of clearance; EU MDR/IVDR apps must publish device details. — [Health apps declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14738291)
- Health Connect: a clear, detailed justification per requested data type explaining user benefit; "Needed for app functionality" is too vague; high-sensitivity categories (reproductive health, substance use, clinical vitals, self-reported symptoms) get heightened scrutiny. Published rejection reasons: insufficient justification, misalignment with approved use cases, bulk-requesting unneeded permissions, insufficient detail on collection/use/sharing. Policy also applies to Wear OS apps. — [Health Connect FAQ](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12991134)

All files access declaration
- Must justify why SAF or MediaStore is insufficient and document the core feature in the store description. — [All files access help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10467955)

UPCOMING forms and tooling
- Declaration forms for READ_CONTACTS (full ongoing contact-list access) and persistent precise location were announced as becoming available in Play Console "before October" 2026; pre-review checks flagging contacts/location permission issues start 27 October 2026; Play policy insights in Android Studio covers the same. — [Android Developers Blog, April 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/04/giving-users-clearer-choice-and-everyone-a-safer-more-trusted-app-ecosystem.html)

### Inferences
- Cross-form common failure pattern (derived from the two published lists plus form requirements): video does not show the declared feature end to end; disclosure shown after the OS prompt; store description does not mention the feature the permission supports; reviewer cannot log in; permission still present in an old bundle on a testing track; SDK added the permission and the developer was unaware.
- A pre-submission tool should therefore check all active tracks' bundles, not only the production candidate.

### Gaps
- Google publishes no rejection-reason lists for SMS/Call Log, All files access, QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES, REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES, exact alarm, full-screen intent, photo/video or FGS declarations; anything stated about those in blogs is anecdotal.
- The exact questions of the photo/video, exact alarm and full-screen intent declarations were not available on the pages retrieved.
- Whether the contacts and fine-location declaration forms are actually live in Play Console as of November 2026 could not be confirmed (only the April 2026 blog's forward-looking statement).

## 6. SDK requirements: SDK Index, responsibility, outdated/non-compliant version blocks, user data rules

### Takeaway
The developer is fully responsible for what third-party SDKs do with user data; SDK collection must be reflected in the Data safety form and consented where needed. Play Console flags SDK versions from the bundle's dependency metadata: policy-violating versions can get releases rejected, and versions with critical issues give a 90-day window after which further releases with that version are blocked.

### Cited Findings
- Developers must ensure third-party SDK code does not cause the app to violate Developer Program Policies; SDK providers must not sell personal and sensitive data from the app; SDK data handling must be secure (HTTPS) and limited to purposes users reasonably expect. — [SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374)
- If an SDK collects personal and sensitive data by default, prominent disclosure and consent may be required and evidence must be supplied within 2 weeks of a Google request. — [SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374)
- Listed prohibited SDK behaviours: linking IMEI with location; linking advertising ID to persistent identifiers or email for ads/analytics; requesting background location for undisclosed or unallowed purposes; transmitting IMEI from read_phone_state without consent; audio/call recording unrelated to app function; downloading executable code (dex/native) or installing apps from outside Play; webview with JavaScript interface loading untrusted content; self-updating the APK; foreground services without valid use case; evading review. — [SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374)
- Apps targeting children may only include SDKs self-certified for child-directed services. — [SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374); [Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- Play Console flags known issues with popular SDKs using the dependencies file in the app; notices appear at the top of the Production page or on the Release overview page. — [Using third-party SDKs in your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10358880)
- Policy-violating SDK version: "new app releases using that SDK version can be rejected"; developer is told to fix User Data compliance, move to a provider-recommended version, or remove the SDK. — [Using third-party SDKs in your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10358880)
- SDK version with critical issues: one further release is allowed temporarily, the SDK must be updated within 90 days of that release, after which no further releases using that version. Outdated versions reported by the SDK owner generate a warning. — [Using third-party SDKs in your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10358880)
- SDK providers use Google Play SDK Console to mark versions outdated; for very old, low-usage versions developers get 90 days to update before new releases with those versions are blocked (announced May 2024). — [Android Developers Blog, May 2024](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2024/05/build-better-safer-sdks-google-play-sdk-console.html)
- Google directs developers to the Google Play SDK Index and to SDK providers' Data safety guidance when completing the Data safety form. — [Data safety form help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)

### Inferences
- Pre-submission check: list every dependency and version, look each up in the Google Play SDK Index for "outdated", "critical issue" or "policy issue" notes, and reconcile each SDK's published Data safety disclosure with the app's form.
- SDKs that forward user content to AI model providers fall under the July 2026 clarification on third-party AI integrations (developer stays responsible).

### Gaps
- The SDK Index page itself (play.google.com/sdks) was not fetched; its current fields were not verified this session.
- A search summary of an older/alternate version of the SDK help page said that with an outdated SDK you can release once but not again; the current page read this session only describes a warning for outdated versions and the 90-day block for critical-issue versions. Treat the stricter reading as unconfirmed.

## 7. Child data rules and EU/regional consent enforced via Play

### Takeaway
Apps targeting children must comply with COPPA/GDPR, must not transmit advertising ID or hardware identifiers from children, must not request location in child-only apps, and may use only approved/self-certified SDKs. EU consent is enforced mainly through Google's ads products (certified CMP for personalised ads), not through a Play permission form.

### Cited Findings
- The app including any APIs or SDKs it uses must comply with COPPA, GDPR and other applicable laws. — [Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- Apps solely targeting children must not transmit AAID, SIM serial, build serial, BSSID, MAC, SSID, IMEI or IMSI, and should not request the AD_ID permission when targeting API 33+. Mixed-audience apps must not transmit those from children or users of unknown age. Apps must not request the device phone number from TelephonyManager. — [Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- Apps solely targeting children may not request location permission or collect/use/transmit precise location; Bluetooth must use Companion Device Manager where supported. — [Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- Collection of personal/sensitive information from children (including via APIs/SDKs) must be disclosed; sensitive data here includes authentication info, microphone and camera sensor data, device data, Android ID and ad usage data; the privacy policy must accurately reflect practices. — [Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- Child-only apps must not include APIs/SDKs not approved for primarily child-directed services; mixed-audience apps may use them only behind a neutral age screen or where they collect no children's data. Ads to children or users of unknown age only via Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs; no interest-based advertising or remarketing. — [Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- Target audience must be declared in PC (Target audience and content); misrepresentation can lead to removal; Target audience, Data safety and IARC answers must be kept accurate. — [Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- Health Connect is not permitted in apps that solely target children. — [Permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16585319)
- From 26 Aug 2026 anonymous chat apps may not target children (Families) and age-restriction/child-safety policies extend to anonymous and random chat apps. — [Policy Deadlines table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780)
- EU User Consent Policy (ads): publishers using AdMob/Ad Manager/AdSense must use a Google-certified CMP integrated with the IAB TCF to serve personalised ads in the EEA and UK (from 16 Jan 2024) and Switzerland (from 31 July 2024); non-certified traffic is limited to non-personalised or limited ads; UMP SDK available for apps; no personalised ads to users flagged as children regardless of consent. — [AdMob Help: certified CMP requirement](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/13554116)
- App Set ID and advertising ID collection require legally valid consent "where required". — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)

### Inferences
- For a child-directed or mixed-audience app the manifest check is stricter: flag AD_ID, any location permission, READ_PHONE_STATE and any SDK not on the Families Self-Certified list.
- EU consent is a prerequisite for ad revenue and for lawful advertising-ID use rather than a Play review gate; a Play rejection would arise indirectly via the User Data policy's requirement to comply with applicable privacy law.

### Gaps
- No Google Play policy page was found that enforces the EU Digital Markets Act on third-party developers; DMA-related consent (Consent Mode for Google ads/analytics services) was not confirmed from an official page in this session.
- The Families policy page showed no effective date; the list of approved "Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs" was not retrieved.

Age Signals API and US state app-store laws
- Google's help page states Texas SB 2420 (App Store Accountability Act) is now in effect after a federal appeals court stayed the December 2025 preliminary injunction; other states' laws take effect later and Play applies age verification per each state's legal effective date. — [Play changes for US state laws](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691)
- Google Play does not mandate that developers use its age features; developers must determine whether and how the laws apply to them. — [Play changes for US state laws](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691)
- Play Age Signals API (beta; recommended library version 0.0.4): for eligible users in applicable states, returns verification/supervision status, age ranges and related signals via a client-side call while the app is open; parental approval for downloads, purchases and "significant changes" is handled by Play; developers define what a significant change is and notify Play on the Age Signals page in PC (no new app version required); a revocation report shows when a parent revokes approval; Play will not disable the app when a parent denies a significant change. — [Play changes for US state laws](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691)
- Texas-specific: apps using Play Billing Library may need to assign age ratings to in-app product SKUs (separate from the app content rating), in PC or via the Publishing API. — [Play changes for US state laws](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691)
- API terms (last modified October 2025): information may be used only "to provide age-appropriate content and experiences in compliance with laws"; not for advertising, marketing, user profiling or analytics; only for apps updated by Google Play; only the requesting app may use the returned information; misuse can end API access and lead to suspension or takedown. The client library itself collects no data. — [Play Age Signals overview](https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/overview)
- Conflicting status reports: a search snippet of an earlier version of Google's help page said the Texas launch was paused under the injunction and listed Utah as effective 7 May 2026 and Louisiana 1 July 2026; a news report claims Utah and Louisiana later moved to 2027 and that live signals began for Texas accounts created after 28 May 2026. — [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/google-play-age-signals-api-global-rollout/). The page as read on 14 Nov 2026 names only Texas as in effect.

### Inferences
- For an app submitted in November 2026 the binding privacy/permission rules are the pre-2027 ones; the 27 January 2027 set should be treated as "fix now, enforced in about ten weeks", since pre-review checks for contacts and location are already running (from 27 Oct 2026 per the April blog).
- Apps targeting API 36 or lower are not yet caught by the location-button and READ_CONTACTS declaration conditions, which are scoped to apps targeting Android 17 (API 37+); the minimum-scope principle still applies to them.
- Age Signals is optional from Play's side but legally relevant for apps with Texas users; if used, the returned age data must be firewalled from analytics and ads pipelines.

- No official statement explaining the move of the contacts/location deadline from 28 Oct 2026 to 27 Jan 2027 was found.
- Both 2026 announcement pages were truncated by the fetch tool; additional privacy-related items may exist beyond those listed.
- Any policy announcement after 15 July 2026 (for example an October 2026 announcement) was not found; the Policy Deadlines table was used as the latest state.
